# Trace / necessity / sufficiency review of the draft

Body count, LaTeX stripped: ~10,680 (rendered citations take it toward 11,600). The cuts below total ~1,585 words.

## A. Ranked issues

**1. The parity thesis is stated more widely than its support (01:28–29; abstract 24–27).** "None of the three is available for any claim about the source" covers sense 2 for all routes. But the second result binds only followable routes, and §2 (02:27–30) and §7 (07:52–54, 80–84) concede that an oracle may escape it. *Fix:* use one canonical sentence: "No claim about the source can be certified by any route from experience, or reliably reached by any followable route, and no one can show without circularity that their route is reliable rather than a whole-record rival."

**2. The structural result does not hold as stated (05:142–152; abstract 31–32; 04:255–256).** (a) Reliability is "answered by the second result", which depends on the route: followable or oracle. (b) Knowledge-first/default (standard 7) is missing from the list. (c) Practical warrant and contextualism are "compatible", which contradicts the abstract's "every route falls short under every standard". *Fix:* answer reliability by the first result applied to the second-order claim (see 3). Add standard 7. Rewrite the abstract: "no route meets any surveyed standard that requires establishing a claim about the source; the standards that do not require it are consistent with the thesis".

**3. The sense-3 claim rests on the wrong result (05:42–45).** It rests on the second result, so it fails for supernatural routes. The general ground: "my source is reliable" is itself a claim about the source (a bias, 03:40–42), so the first result denies its certification by any route. Bootstrapping (05:45–48) and Descartes' circle (07:54–58) become illustrations, not premises.

**4. The claim that the first and third results bind supernatural routes needs one explicit step (02:25–30, 02:39–40).** The first result assumes a pronouncement "depends on the record". For an oracle this holds only if its deliverances enter the record (03:24–25 says so; §2 does not). The third result binds an oracle only if its deliverance reaches the believer as experience a deceiver world can reproduce, which the traditions concede (04:130–141). *Fix:* add one sentence at 02:27 and cite the theophany concessions as the instance.

**5. The ideal-knower concession is inconsistent (02:58 vs 02:60–64, 07:80–84, 01:48–49).** 02:58 says even an ideal knower is helpless against whole-record rivals. 02:64 and 07:80–84 say a perfect knower could know "the truth about the source". *Fix:* the concession covers behavior (second result) only. God's self-knowledge does not work from a record and lies outside the argument. Rewrite 07:80–84 to say so and delete 02:60–64.

**6. Width of field: wrong level (01:39, 04:112).** "Does not converge on one source" is wrong: under parity no field converges on a source. 06:66–73 has it right: perception converges on behavior and content, religious experience diverges in content. *Fix:* "converge on one account" in both places. 04:36 has a similar slip ("disagree about the source"; near-ties disagree about the unobserved).

**7. The claim is not worded the same everywhere.** Abstract and intro use "known" (three senses). 08:3–5 uses "established". The §3 table has no row for sense 3 or for rivals that differ on the unobserved, and the §5 summary uses "transient underdetermination" (05:108) and "near-tie result" (05:145). *Fix:* use the canonical sentence from 1 verbatim in abstract, 01:19, 03 (table caption or opening) and 08:3. Add two table rows. Replace off-vocabulary terms with "rival that differs on the unobserved".

**8. Route numbering is missing.** 07:40 and 07:48 cite "route 2" and "route 3", but §4 never numbers routes. The abstract lists eight routes as "nine" and omits innate belief and inner witness. *Fix:* number the §4 subsections as in spine §4A and correct the abstract list.

**9. Spelling (style: American).** sceptic/scepticism at 01:22, 01:68, 07:3, 07:15, 07:62, 07:98, 07:107 (against "skeptical" at 04:76, 07:103); favour 07:5, 07:16; defence 06:39, 06:45; travellers 05:102; per cent 03:82.

**10. Elevated thinkers and defensive wording.** Elevation: "Hume saw that" 04:81 (use "Hume holds"); full-name introductions only in §6 (John Hick, Robert McKim, Alan Race, Alvin Plantinga, David Basinger, Keith DeRose, Stephen Law, William Alston), so use surnames; "The circle has not loosened since" 07:56. Defensive: 04:140–141, 05:39–40 ("concession costs nothing"), 07:101–102, 08:29–30. Novelty claims made in the body: 04:37–39, 04:236 (keep in intro only).

**11. Math register (minor).** "Church–Turing thesis" 02:21–22; "made exact" 02:40; "probability zero" 02:57, 03:77 (say "agree on which events can never occur").

**"Knows" check:** no violation. Check 05:120–121: "the believer… may be said to know" should be "the contextualist may say the believer knows". The author is not discussed; the companion paper is named four ways (companion/first/earlier/"algorithmic agnosticism calls"). Use one.
