# Argument spine (v2.1): The Mind of God

**Working title:** *The Mind of God: Algorithmic Agnosticism and Religious Belief*

**Builds on:** C. S. Taylor, *Algorithmic Agnosticism: Why No Theory of Everything Can Be Known to
Be Final* (v1.1), https://github.com/extrafox/algorithmic-agnosticism. Cited below as **AA**.

**Status.** Discussion draft for review. It incorporates eight research strands and an adversarial
review (`research/lit/01–08`). Nothing is drafted yet. Every element of the paper must appear here
with its dependencies, and anything not on the spine is cut.

**Allusion.** Hawking, *A Brief History of Time* (1988), final paragraph: a complete theory "would be
the ultimate triumph of human reason — for then we would know the mind of God". The wording is
verified against the 1989 Bantam text; the first-edition page (p. 175) is to be checked against a
physical copy. Paul Davies, *The Mind of God* (1992), takes up the theme. A footnote can use
Hawking's 2014 gloss: "…if there were a God, which there isn't" (lit/01).

### v2.1 (after author review)
- **"Knows" is not asserted.** A believer may *hold the truth*, and on externalist accounts may even be said to know it, but no believer can establish that they hold it. This is AA's point applied to a person: a method may already output the correct final theory, and nothing tells it so. The paper reports the externalist verdict and takes no side in the internalist/externalist dispute (§0, standard 2).
- **New route 3b, first-person revelation (theophany)**, separated from mystical experience (3a) and from transmitted revelation (2b).
- **New route 7, miracles and signs** (Hume, *iʿjāz*, the resurrection appearances).
- **Research strands 09 and 10** verified the new material. The traditions themselves concede that a deceiver can reproduce the experience, so the C3(a) rivals are the traditions' own, not philosophers' inventions.

### What changed from v1, and why

- **The core claim is now a parity thesis about the ultimate source** (§0). The adversarial review
  (lit/08) showed that the v1 claim, read literally, also defeats ordinary perception. It was
  therefore either global scepticism or special pleading against religion. AA's own levels resolve
  this:
  - claims about *behaviour* (how experience continues) can be settled;
  - claims about the *source* of experience cannot be settled by anyone, whether theistic,
    naturalistic or sceptical.

  This is also the author's own framing: religious belief is unknowable in the same way that final
  theories are unknowable.
- **The third sense of "known" is repaired** (§0). Read literally, "no one can know that they know"
  is false on externalist terms. It becomes: *no one can establish, without epistemic circularity,
  that their source is reliable rather than a rival that fits the same record* (lit/02, lit/08).
- **A worldview is mapped to a class of worlds**, not a single model (§3). Holding a worldview is
  then holding a bias (AA, lemma L). Doctrines that describe but predict nothing (the Trinity) are
  handled (§6).
- **Rivals are classified precisely** (§3). Madness, a deceiver and a simulation agree with the
  *whole* record, so they are C3(a) rivals, not C1 rivals.
- **Certitude is corrected** (§4A, route 5). Aquinas's "more certain simply" is a claim about the
  cause of faith, which makes it externalist (standard 2). His "less certain for us" concedes the
  paper's point. Vatican I's canon 2.1 is named as the opponent of C1. The distinction itself is
  Newman's.
- **The oracle escape is corrected** (§2). AA's relativized C2 holds for deterministic methods only.
  The theist's reply is divine veracity, Descartes' move, which brings Descartes' circle with it. Lots
  and the Urim are treated as oracles. *Istikhāra* is a prayer asking God to choose, not a chance
  procedure, and Q 5:3 forbids drawing lots.
- **The taxonomy is refined** against the surveys (lit/02, lit/03):
  - testimony is split into transmission and source (*tawātur* certifies at most the transmission);
  - the inner witness of the Spirit is separated from the *sensus divinitatis*;
  - faith without belief is added;
  - hybrid routes are noted;
  - three standards are added: contextualism, knowledge-first, and Mīmāṃsā's *svataḥ prāmāṇya*;
  - the grid's completeness claim is softened.
- **Precedents and novelty are recorded** (§8, from lit/06). These include DeRose (2018), Schellenberg
  (2007), Hick (1989) and McKim (2001).

## 0. The claim

**Core claim: a parity thesis about the source.** No claim about the ultimate source of human
experience can be known to be true by any route from that experience. This holds whether the claim
is theistic (God), naturalistic (matter and law) or sceptical (a deceiver or simulator). Religious
worldviews are no worse placed than a theory of everything, and no better. "Known" has three senses
here:

1. *certified*: established on the basis of what can be experienced;
2. *reliably reached*: arrived at by a route guaranteed to settle on the truth, whatever reality is
   like;
3. *non-circularly shown to be known*: the believer can establish, without presupposing the answer,
   that their source is reliable rather than a rival that fits the same record.

**What is specific to religion** (§3, C′; §5):

- **The width of the surviving field.** Perception is public, repeatable and cross-checked, so it
  narrows its field sharply. Religious experience across traditions does not converge on one source.
- **Some traditions claim more than parity permits.** Examples: Vatican I's natural certainty of
  God; *tawātur* as necessary knowledge; *kashf* as *yaqīn*. The paper rejects these explicitly.

**Reach.** The claim holds for every route to belief, natural and supernatural (§4A), and under every
standard surveyed (§4B), for every worldview, naturalism included.

**Not claimed.** The paper states each of these explicitly:

- that God does not exist;
- that religious belief is irrational;
- that faith is illegitimate or irresponsible;
- that a believer cannot hold the truth. In the good case a believer may hold it, and on externalist accounts may even be said to know it. The paper does not assert that they know, and takes no side in that dispute. What it denies is that anyone can **establish** that they hold the truth;
- that every worldview fits the record equally well (the record narrows the field);
- that a perfect knower could not know (the limit is on the human inquirer, §6);
- that ordinary perceptual knowledge fails (claims about behaviour can be settled);
- anything about the nature of God.

**Position.** Agnosticism in Huxley's sense: neither affirm nor deny, and "do not pretend that
conclusions are certain which are not demonstrated or demonstrable" (*Collected Essays* V, p. 246;
coinage pp. 239–240). Here it is a justified conclusion about the source of experience, held
equally against the theist's, the naturalist's and the sceptic's claims to know.

## 1. Terms (from lit/01)

- **Atheism** has two senses (Draper, SEP "Atheism and Agnosticism", rev. 2022):
  - psychological: lack of belief in God (Flew's "simply not a theist", 1972);
  - propositional: the claim that God does not exist.
- **Agnosticism**, in two senses:
  - Huxley's method;
  - the thesis of §0.

  Avoid the label "strong agnosticism", which Draper uses for a different thesis.
- **Worldview** (after Smart; it includes secular naturalism) is the unit of analysis, and the
  counterpart of a theory of everything. Use **religious tradition** (W. C. Smith's "cumulative
  tradition") for historical communities, and James's **religious hypothesis** as the counterpart
  of a theory.
- **Route to belief** and **standard of knowledge**: §4.

## 2. AA's two premises, in philosophical language

AA has **two** premises (an earlier "third axiom" was a mistake). The paper never treats God as
computed.

- **P1, finite experience.** Everything a person can observe or experience, and every record of it,
  is finite. This is a claim about the believer's resources, not about God or reality.
- **P2, followable routes.** Every route a person can actually follow, articulate or rely on can be
  followed step by step, or described as a stochastic procedure.
- **P2\*, the oracle escape.** Suppose a route has non-effective powers (revelation, grace, or lots
  and Urim read as divine oracles; Prov 16:33).
  - C1 and C3 still hold, for routes of any kind.
  - For **deterministic** routes, C2 still holds unless the route's power exceeds anything the
    reality it confronts could match.
  - The theist's exit is **divine veracity**: God "can neither deceive nor be deceived" (*Dei
    Filius* ch. 3), which excludes the rival worlds. This is Descartes' move. The exclusion is a
    bias restated as a belief about the world (lemma L), and certifying it is circular. Concede that
    *if* the rivals are excluded, convergence is possible, but it cannot be shown (C1).

**Structural fact.** Only C2 depends on P2. C1 and C3 hold for routes of any kind.

## 3. The mapping

| AA (science) | Religious counterpart |
|---|---|
| Reality: the source of all records, nature left open | God as the ultimate source or ground of all records (or a tradition's ultimate ground), nature left open. It is not God identified with the universe, so classical theism fits and this is not pantheism |
| The finite record | The finite human record: scripture, testimony, history, nature, and each person's own experiences |
| A model (one computable world) | A **worldview corresponds to a class of worlds**: everything compatible with its doctrines. "Certify W" means "certify that the actual world is in W's class". **Holding a worldview is holding a bias** (lemma L) |
| A method of selection | A route to belief (§4A) |
| "Known to be final" | "Known to be true" (the three senses of §0) |
| **C1** no method certifies | No route, natural or supernatural, certifies a worldview, because the record never entails it |
| **C2** no usable method is guaranteed to settle | No followable route is guaranteed to settle on the truth, and the believer cannot rule out a reality where their route fails |
| **C3(a)** permanent rivals that agree on the whole record | Madness, drugs, a deceiver, a simulation. Theism versus naturalism, *if* they never diverge (Hick's "religious ambiguity", 1989, p. 12). Doctrinal variants that differ in no possible experience |
| **C1/C2** transient rivals that differ only on the unobserved | Theism versus naturalism *if* they diverge eschatologically (standard 5) |
| **C3(b)** chance hypotheses agreeing on null sets | Providential versus pure-chance accounts of the same random events. Prayer efficacy (STEP: 52% versus 51%). Divine action through quantum indeterminacy (Russell; Murphy) in one sentence |
| **C′, near-ties** | The record does real work: some worldviews conflict with it. Among the survivors, choosing by faith, tradition, authority, beauty or community is imposed (James; Pascal; Kierkegaard) |
| **T4** compression still predicts | "By their fruits": worldviews can have practical and testable consequences, so they have value without certainty |

**The three levels of identity** (AA D7):

- *formulation*: the doctrinal wording;
- *predicted experience*: behaviour, which can be settled, as in perception;
- *providential probabilities*: chance.

**The source** lies beyond all three, and is permanently underdetermined.

**Precision rule.** Each route cell states which kind of rival defeats it: a whole-record rival
(C3(a)) or a rival that differs only on the unobserved (C1/C2).

**Hybrid routes.** Several routes combine others. Calvin combines 4a and 4b. Halevi combines 2a
and 2b. Al-Ghazālī's "light God cast into the breast" (*Munqidh*) restores trust in reason by 4b.
A hybrid is answered by each of its components. Revelation passes from 3b (to the witness) to 2b (to everyone else).

**Non-propositional knowing** (apophatic theology; Advaita; Sufi *maʿrifa*) is handled in §6.

### 4B. Standards of knowledge

| Standard | Holders | AA result |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Certification** (classical internalism) | Clifford (1877/79); Vatican I's natural certainty | **C1**, for any route |
| **2. Reliability or proper function** (externalism) | Plantinga (*Warranted Christian Belief* pp. 188, 191, 498); Alston; reliabilists; Aquinas's certainty-by-cause | In the good case the believer **may hold the truth**, and the externalist may call this knowledge. The paper does not assert or deny that. **Sense 3 is not met:** no one can establish non-circularly that their source is reliable rather than a rival that fits the same record (bootstrapping: Vogel 2000; Cohen 2002; Alston 1986, 1991). | **5. Future certification** (eschatological verification) | Hick, "Theology and Verification" (*Theology Today* 17(1), 1960, 12–31); critics including Tooley (1976) | **AA's reply to transient underdetermination.** No finite stage certifies; no one can know when the open phase has ended (C2, prefix form); part of the underdetermination is permanent (C3) |
| **6. Contextual standards** | DeRose (1995) | In ordinary contexts the believer may "know". The paper's question is posed at the standard of the source, the philosopher's context, and there C1–C3 apply. A contextualist can accept both |
| **7. Knowledge-first; default validity** | Williamson; Mīmāṃsā's *svataḥ prāmāṇya* (valid unless defeated) | Akin to standard 2. A default that holds unless defeated is a constraint (C′). **Structural result** (softened from v1). For every standard in the surveys, the answering AA result
depends on the **standard**, not the **route**. The routes supply the instances: how each enters the
record, and which kind of rival it faces. The paper claims coverage of the standards surveyed, not
logical completeness.

## 5. Religious diversity as underdetermination

- **The rival-worldviews point** at the scale of traditions:
  - Hick, *An Interpretation of Religion* (1989), p. 12 ("religious ambiguity"); the pluralistic
    hypothesis, ch. 14;
  - McKim, *Religious Ambiguity and Religious Diversity* (2001).
- **Exclusivism, inclusivism and pluralism** are imposed choices (C′). Sources: Race (1983) and the
  usual typology; Plantinga, "Pluralism: A Defense of Religious Exclusivism" (1995); Basinger (2002).
- **Peer disagreement** (Feldman; Kelly; Christensen; King) is a defeater that cuts across every
  route.
- **Parity.** DeRose's "Son of Great Pumpkin" ("Voodoo Epistemology", 1999) asks whether rival
  believers can use Plantinga's defence as well as he can. That is the paper's parity point in the
  literature's own terms.
- **The evil-god challenge** (Law, *Religious Studies* 46, 2010; replies from Weaver 2015, Collins
  2019 and 2024, Hendricks 2018, Lancaster-Thomas 2018 and 2020). Claim only that cheap rivals are not
  strawmen, not that the two hypotheses are exactly symmetric.
- **The width of the field** is what is specific to religion: the traditions do not converge on one
  source.

## 6. Objections

1. **"This is global scepticism."** Reply: no. It is parity at the source level. Claims about
   behaviour, including ordinary perception, can be settled. Claims about the source cannot, for
   theists, naturalists and sceptics alike. Alston and al-Ghazālī use this parity *for* theism; the
   paper accepts the parity and denies the conclusion.
2. **Category error.** "God is not an object in the record." Reply: agreed. God corresponds to the
   source of the record. Every *claim about* God enters the record.
3. **"Faith is not a method."** Reply: then it is an imposed choice (C′). If faith is offered as
   knowledge, C1 applies to any route.
4. **Non-descriptive or non-predictive doctrine.** Three cases:
   - (a) Non-descriptive language (Wittgensteinian fideism; D. Z. Phillips): no claim is made, so the
     question does not arise.
   - (b) **Descriptive but not predictive** (the Trinity; divine simplicity; analogy; apophasis):
     these enter only through testimony (route 2), and C1 applies there. The paper admits that part
     of this answer is trivial: the record cannot bear on them at all. Aquinas agrees that the
     Trinity cannot be known by natural reason (ST I q.32 a.1).
   - (c) **Non-propositional knowing** (apophatic union; Advaita *jñāna*; *maʿrifa*): if it is not
     knowledge *that*, it makes no truth claim about a worldview. If it is offered as one, it is
     route 3.
5. **The oracle escape and divine veracity.** Reply: §2. C1 and C3 bind it; the veracity exit is
   Descartes' circle.
6. **"This is Hume's, Kant's or Huxley's old point."** Reply: the conclusion has precedents (§8). The
   additions are C2, C3, the reach to supernatural routes, the source-level parity, and the grid.
7. **From the atheist side.** "Agnosticism is too generous" (Russell's teapot). Reply: the claim
   binds naturalism equally. Atheism as lack of belief is compatible with it. Atheism as denial is a
   claim about the source, and falls under it.
8. **"Only God knows."** Reply: concede it. AA's ideal-agent concession applies. The limit is on the
   human inquirer.
9. **Theistic idealism (Berkeley).** Berkeley holds that God causes our ideas (*Principles* §§29–30,
   146–149; *Dialogues* II–III). Reply: the same as AA's reply to Müller. Affirming the source goes
   beyond the record; this is an instance of the claim, not a counterexample.
10. **Divine hiddenness and skeptical theism** (Schellenberg 1993, 2007; Wykstra; Bergmann).
    Hiddenness tries to *refute* theism, which is stronger than this paper. Skeptical theism
    undercuts knowledge of revelation (Wielenberg; Maitzen), which supports route 2b.

2. **Algorithmic agnosticism without mathematics.** The two premises, P2\*, and the three results;
   the three levels of identity, and the source beyond them.
3. **The mapping.** God as the source; a worldview as a class of worlds; the two kinds of rival.
4. **Routes to belief.** Each route's entry into the record and its rival type.
5. **Standards of knowledge.** Each answered; the structural result.
6. **Religious diversity and the width of the field.**
7. **Objections**, led by global scepticism.
8. **Agnosticism, justified.** What it permits (faith as a choice; the fruits of worldviews) and what

## 8. Precedents and contributions (from lit/06)

**Closest in conclusion.** The paper must cite these and must not claim to have originated religious
agnosticism:

- DeRose, "Delusions of Knowledge Concerning God's Existence" (2018): hardly anyone knows whether
  God exists, argued from religious experience;
- Schellenberg, *The Wisdom to Doubt* (2007), and his "we are early" theme;
- Hick (1989) and McKim (2001) on religious ambiguity;
- Oppy (2006);
- Le Poidevin, *Agnosticism: A Very Short Introduction* (2010).

**Neighbours.**

- Harrison (*Religious Studies*, 2025) on underdetermination in science and theology (historical,
  and running the other way).
- Langtry (2011), Gwiazda (2009), Miller (2015), Gagnon (2013) and Philipse (2017) on Swinburne's
  simplicity. None argues that simplicity is relative to the choice of machine.
- Gödel-based theology (Grim; Turner; Driessen and Suarez) concerns God's nature, not the limits of
  the inquirer.

**Not found:**

- "algorithmic agnosticism" in theology;
- formal learning theory applied to religious belief;
- Kolmogorov or Solomonoff simplicity applied to theism in a peer-reviewed venue;
- simulation or brain-in-a-vat arguments aimed at knowledge from religious experience.

**Contributions, "to our knowledge":**

- deriving religious agnosticism from formal results (C1–C3);
- the source-level parity with theories of everything;
- the non-circular second-order result against externalism;
- simplicity relative to the choice of machine, against Swinburne;
- closing the oracle escape (P2\*);
- prophecy as novel prediction;
- the routes × standards grid.

**Positioning.**

- *Against Schellenberg:* a permanent limit, not "we are early".
- *Against DeRose:* every route, not one, plus the second-order result.

## 10. Style and venue

- Write in the language of religion and philosophy, not mathematics. Cite AA's results; do not
  reprove them.
- Claims broad in reach and narrow in content. Each qualification is stated once, where its claim is
  stated. No thinker gets elevated status, and the author is not discussed.
- Use the tradition's own terms, with transliteration. Quotations are verified (lit/03 lists the
  checked passages and the corrected references: Kuzari I.11, *Sotah* 48b, *Yesodei haTorah* 1:6).
- **Target venue:** *International Journal for Philosophy of Religion*. It states no word limit,
  accepts unaffiliated authors, charges no required fee, allows disclosed AI use, and has a
