## 4. Paraphrases that misstate the source
1. **06:41–42.** Plantinga (RBG p. 78) says a natural tendency "to see his hand in the world around us", not a tendency "to believe in him". He also puts it as something the Reformed epistemologist "may concur with Calvin" in holding.
2. **06:44–45.** DeRose's "easy question" is whether Plantinga is *committed* to the bizarre beliefs being properly basic. It is not "whether any belief whatever can be basic"; that is the original objection.
3. **04:53–55.** In Kuzari I.8 the King (not Halevi's Rabbi) lays down a general condition on any revelation; I.88 is the King conceding Sinai. Suggested wording: "Halevi's King demands … (I.8) and grants that Sinai, 'seen by thousands, cannot be denied' (I.88)."
4. **04:209.** James says "passional nature" decides, not the "will".
5. **07:13–15.** AA §6 proves permanence for extensionally equal formulations and for chance hypotheses that agree on probability-zero events. Applying it to God / matter / deceiver is this paper's own extension, so flag it as such ("cf.").
6. **Minor.** 04:185, Aquinas compares faith with all three intellectual virtues, not only science. 07:6–8, Alston's parity of mystical perception is in the 1991 book (ch. 3), not the 1986 paper.
