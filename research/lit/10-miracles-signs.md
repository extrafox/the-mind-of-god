# Lit 10: Miracles and signs as a route to belief

Miracles and signs offered as public evidence: route 1 for eyewitnesses, route 2a/2b (testimony) for
everyone else. **[V]** = verified this session; **[U]** = not verified.

## 1. Hume, "Of Miracles" (*Enquiry* §10, 1748)

Hume Texts Online, https://davidhume.org/texts/e/10 **[V]**.

- E 10.4: "A wise man, therefore, proportions his belief to the evidence."
- E 10.12: "A miracle is a violation of the laws of nature"; so the proof against one "is as entire as
  any argument from experience can possibly be imagined."
- E 10.12 n. (Note K): "a transgression of a law of nature by a particular volition of the Deity, or by
  the interposition of some invisible agent".
- E 10.13: no testimony suffices unless "its falsehood would be more miraculous, than the fact, which
  it endeavours to establish"; "I weigh the one miracle against the other … and always reject the
  greater miracle." The brief's wording "unless the testimony be of such a kind" is the standard text
  but was not confirmed word for word **[U]**.
- Part II's four considerations:
  1. E 10.15: no miracle is "attested by a sufficient number of men" of good sense and integrity;
  2. E 10.16: "the passion of surprize and wonder";
  3. E 10.20: such reports "abound among ignorant and barbarous nations";
  4. E 10.24: "not only the miracle destroys the credit of testimony, but the testimony destroys
     itself" (rival religions' miracles cancel one another).
- E 10.41: "all prophecies are real miracles, and as such only, can be admitted as proofs of any
  revelation".

**Evidence:** testimony only.

**Rivals:** deception, error, and the love of wonder. These explain the *reports*.

**Spine:** P1 exactly. For the hearer, Hume's rivals are whole-record rivals (C3(a)). The fourth
consideration anticipates §5's width of the field.

## 2. The modern debate

- **Earman**, *Hume's Abject Failure: The Argument Against Miracles* (Oxford UP, 2000) **[V]**. A
  Bayesian argument that Part I fails: enough independent witnesses can overcome a low prior.
- **Swinburne**, *The Concept of Miracle* (Macmillan, 1970), DOI 10.1007/978-1-349-00776-9 **[V]**.
  His definition, "a non-repeatable counter-instance to a law of nature", is quoted from SEP. Also
  *The Resurrection of God Incarnate* (Clarendon, 2003) **[V]**, a full Bayesian case.
- **T. & L. McGrew**, "The Argument from Miracles: A Cumulative Case for the Resurrection of Jesus of
  Nazareth", in Craig & Moreland (eds), *Blackwell Companion to Natural Theology* (Wiley-Blackwell,
  2009), 593–662, DOI 10.1002/9781444308334.ch11 **[V]**. They set the prior aside and argue that the
  likelihood ratio is "top heavy". That description is SEP's; the chapter's own wording is **[U]**.
- **SEP "Miracles"**, by Timothy McGrew and Robert Larmer. First published 2010; substantive revision
  7 May 2024 **[V]**. Its definition: "an event that exceeds the productive power of nature". The 2010
  version was by Michael Levine.
- **Mackie**, *The Miracle of Theism* (Clarendon, 1982), ch. 1 **[V]**. The chapter title and an
  exact summary are **[U]**.
- **Levine**, *Hume and the Problem of Miracles: A Solution* (Kluwer, 1989), DOI
  10.1007/978-94-009-2245-7 **[V]**.
- **Fogelin**, *A Defense of Hume on Miracles* (Princeton UP, 2003) **[V]**. Hume excludes miracle
  testimony in practice, not a priori; Fogelin argues against Earman.

**Spine:** the debate runs under standard 3. Its disputes are mostly about priors: low for Hume and
Mackie; higher on Swinburne's theistic background; set aside by the McGrews. That is C′. Likelihood
ratios show that the record does real work, but no finite ratio certifies (C1).

## 3. Resurrection appearances as public signs

- **Wright**, *The Resurrection of the Son of God* (SPCK/Fortress, 2003) **[V]**. The empty tomb
  together with the appearances is necessary and sufficient to explain the Christian change in
  resurrection belief. He rejects hallucination and cognitive dissonance as explanations.
- **Licona**, *The Resurrection of Jesus: A New Historiographical Approach* (IVP Academic, 2010)
  **[V]**. "Historical bedrock" facts, with hypotheses scored by scope, power, plausibility and how
  little is *ad hoc*.
- **Critics:**
  - **Ehrman**, *How Jesus Became God* (HarperOne, 2014) **[V]**: a miracle is by definition the
    least probable event, so historians cannot establish one. The details of the 2006 Craig debate
    are **[U]**.
  - **Martin**, "Why the Resurrection Is Initially Improbable", *Philo* 1(1) (1998), 63–73, DOI
    10.5840/philo1998116 **[V]**: a low prior.
  - **Carrier**, in Price & Lowder (eds), *The Empty Tomb* (Prometheus, 2005) **[V]**: a "two-body"
    reading of Paul, the empty tomb as legend, the appearances as visions. The chapter title and
    pages are **[U]**.
- **Earliest record:** 1 Cor 15:3–8.

**Evidence:** first-hand only for the claimed witnesses. For everyone later it is a finite set of
texts.

**Rivals:** visions, legend and the wrong tomb all explain the *texts*, so they are C3(a) for us. The
rivals differ on first-century facts (C1), but those facts are out of reach.

**Spine:** both sides rank explanations of one finite record (P1). The near-tie is broken by priors
and explanatory virtues (C′).

## 4. *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān* and the *taḥaddī*

The verses, in Saheeh International via the quran.com API **[V]**:

- Q 2:23–24: "produce a sūrah the like thereof … and you will never be able to". This is a prediction.
- Q 10:38: "bring forth a sūrah like it".
- Q 11:13: "bring ten sūrahs like it".
- Q 17:88: "If mankind and the jinn gathered … they could not produce the like of it".
- Q 52:34: "let them produce a statement like it".

**Classical theorists:**
- al-Bāqillānī (d. 1013), *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān* **[V]**;
- al-Jurjānī (d. 1078 per Iranica; 1081 is **[U]**), *Dalāʾil al-iʿjāz* **[V]**;
- *ṣarfa*, the doctrine that God prevented imitation: al-Murtaḍā held it and ʿAbd al-Jabbār rejected
  it **[V]**. Attributing it to al-Naẓẓām is standard but **[U]**.

**Reference works and studies:**
- *Encyclopaedia of the Qurʾān*, "Inimitability", DOI 10.1163/1875-3922_q3_eqcom_00093 **[V]**; the
  author is **[U]**.
- Vasalou, "The Miraculous Eloquence of the Qur'an", *JQS* 4(2) (2002), 23–53, DOI
  10.3366/jqs.2002.4.2.23 **[V]**: the opponents' "failure to do so must surely attest to the divine
  origin of the text."
- Kermani, *Gott ist schön* (Beck, 1999); *God Is Beautiful* (Polity, 2015) **[V]**.

**Evidence:** two parts.
- (i) The eloquence is first-hand, but only for a hearer competent in Arabic.
- (ii) The inference to a divine source needs a historical premise: the challenge has never been met.
  That premise, like the claim that the text has come down intact, is transmitted (*tawātur*).
- No published source frames (ii) as testimony, so it is the paper's own analysis.

**Rivals:**
- human genius;
- *ṣarfa*, which concedes that the text could be imitated in principle;
- circularity: the text sets the standard for "like it". The circularity charge is attributed to
  Madigan **[U]**.

**Spine:** this fits 2a/2b exactly. *Tawātur* certifies at most that no one has imitated the text,
not why. "Unmatched" generalises from a finite record of attempts (C1). Q 2:24 falls under prophecy
as novel prediction (K1).

## 5. Other signs (NRSVUE, Bible Gateway **[V]**)

- **Exodus 7–11.**
  - "multiply my signs and wonders" (7:3), so that "the Egyptians shall know that I am the Lord"
    (7:5).
  - The magicians match the early signs by "secret arts" (7:11, 22; wording **[U]**).
  - At the gnats they fail and say "This is the finger of God!" (8:18–19).
  - Pharaoh stays unmoved.
  - The text itself stages the rival explanation (technique) and shows evidence failing to compel.
- **1 Kings 18.**
  - "How long will you go limping with two different opinions?" (v. 21);
  - "the god who answers by fire is indeed God" (v. 24);
  - "so that this people may know" (v. 37).
  - A designed public test with a controlled rival. It is first-hand on the mountain and testimony
    for every reader since.
- **John 20:30–31.**
  - "many other signs … not written in this book"; "these are written so that you may continue to
    believe" (or "come to believe").
  - The tradition states P1 itself: a finite, selected record for those who were not there.
