## 1. Huxley, Spencer, Kant

- **Coinage.** Huxley, "Agnosticism" (1889), in *Collected Essays* V, *Science and Christian
  Tradition* (London: Macmillan, 1894), pp. 209–262 [V; Clark Univ. Huxley File]. At
  the Metaphysical Society (founded 1869), he says: "So I took thought, and invented what I conceived
  to be the appropriate title of 'agnostic.'" It was "suggestively antithetic to the 'gnostic' of
  Church history" (pp. 239–240) [V]. The essay itself does not mention the Pauline "unknown God."
- **The principle**, at p. 246 [V]:
  - "Agnosticism, in fact, is not a creed, but a method."
  - Positive form: "In matters of the intellect, follow your reason as far as it will take you,
    without regard to any other consideration."
  - Negative form: "In matters of the intellect do not pretend that conclusions are certain which
    are not demonstrated or demonstrable."
- **Huxley, "Agnosticism and Christianity"** (1889), *CE* V, pp. 309–365 [V], at p. 310 [V]:
  - Agnosticism is "not properly described as a 'negative' creed, nor indeed as a creed of any kind."
  - Its principle: "it is wrong for a man to say that he is certain of the objective truth of any
    proposition unless he can produce evidence which logically justifies that certainty. This is what
    Agnosticism asserts."
- **First periodical publication.** *The Nineteenth Century* 25 (Feb. 1889): 169–194 and (June
  1889): 937–964 **[U]**. US reprints: *Popular Science Monthly* 34–35 (1889) [V].
- **Spencer.** *First Principles* (London: Williams & Norgate, 1862; "Second thousand" 1863), Part I,
  "The Unknowable," §14 [V; Gutenberg #55046, 1863 printing]. The basis for reconciling
  religion and science is "that the Power which the Universe manifests to us is utterly inscrutable."
  Page numbers are [U].
- **Kant**, *Critique of Pure Reason*, B xxx (Preface to 2nd ed., 1787). Two translations:
  - Kemp Smith (1929): "I have therefore found it necessary to deny knowledge, in order to make room
    for faith" [V2].
  - Meiklejohn: "abolish knowledge, to make room for belief" [V; Gutenberg #4280].
  - German: "Ich mußte also das Wissen aufheben, um zum Glauben Platz zu bekommen" (AA III, 19)
    [U against scan]. Note that *aufheben* is ambiguous.

## 2. Contemporary definitions

- **Draper**, "Atheism and Agnosticism," *SEP*: first published 2 Aug. 2017, substantive revision 22
  Mar. 2022 [V]. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/atheism-agnosticism/
  - §1, atheism. *Psychological* sense: "the psychological state of lacking the belief that God
    exists." *Propositional* sense: "the proposition that God does not exist." Draper recommends that
    "philosophers ought to construe atheism as the proposition that God does not exist" [V].
  - §2, agnosticism. *Psychological* sense: one who "has entertained the proposition that there is a
    God but believes neither that it is true nor that it is false." *Epistemological* sense: "the
    position that neither theism nor atheism is known," which the entry adopts [V].
  - Huxley's principle is "similar to (though weaker than) what we now call 'evidentialism'" [V].
  - "Agnostic atheists": Draper notes "the symmetry between this position and fideism" [V]. This is
    useful for §6.6 of the spine.
  - §7, "strong agnosticism": withholding judgment "is rationally required" [V].
- **Flew**, "The Presumption of Atheism," *Canadian Journal of Philosophy* 2(1) (1972): 29–46,
  doi:10.1080/00455091.1972.10716861 [V Crossref]. Reprinted in *The Presumption of Atheism and Other
  Philosophical Essays* (London: Elek/Pemberton, 1976) [V title; publisher U]. "Atheist" means "not
  someone who positively asserts the non-existence of God; but someone who is simply not a theist"
  [V; OL scan of 1976 ed.; page U]. Flew calls this "negative" atheism.
- **Positive/negative** as standard labels: Michael Martin, *Atheism: A Philosophical Justification*
  (Philadelphia: Temple UP, 1990). "Someone who is a positive atheist is by necessity a negative
  atheist, but not conversely" [V; OL scan; page U].
- **Strong/weak** is the popular synonym pair (Bullivant, cited in Draper §1) [V].
- **Oppy**, *Atheism and Agnosticism* (Cambridge Elements in the Philosophy of Religion; CUP, 2018),
  doi:10.1017/9781108555340 [V Crossref]. Per Draper, Oppy uses "agnostic" in "the psychological
  sense of someone who suspends judgment" [V]. Oppy's exact wording is **[U]**.

## 3. The unit of analysis

- ***Weltanschauung*.** Dilthey, "Die Typen der Weltanschauung und ihre Ausbildung in den
  metaphysischen Systemen" (1911), in *Gesammelte Schriften* VIII, *Weltanschauungslehre* [V title
  and volume; OL scans].
- **Ninian Smart** names the study of religion "worldview analysis" [V], in *Religion and the Western Mind* (Albany: SUNY, 1987) [V; page U].
  - Dimensions of religion: six in *The Religious Experience of Mankind* (1969), seven in *Dimensions
    of the Sacred* (Berkeley: U California Press, 1996) [V2].
  - See also *Worldviews: Crosscultural Explorations of Human Beliefs* (New York: Scribner, 1983)
    [U publisher].
  - It explicitly includes secular ideologies.
- **W. C. Smith**, *The Meaning and End of Religion* (New York: Macmillan, 1962/63) [V; OL scans].
  - "The term 'religion' is confusing, unnecessary, and distorting" (p. 48 in the 1964 Mentor ed.
    [V2]).
  - He replaces it with "faith" and "cumulative tradition." The latter is "the entire mass of overt
    objective data that constitute the historical deposit" of a community's past religious life [V2,
    many citers; page U].
- **Hick**, *An Interpretation of Religion: Human Responses to the Transcendent* (Macmillan/Yale UP,
  1989) [V; OL scans].
  - "The Real" is his neutral term for the ultimate. The Real *an sich* is distinguished from its
    *personae* and *impersonae* (ch. 14–15) [chapter numbers U].
  - "The Religious Ambiguity of the Universe" is a chapter heading (ch. 5) [V heading; chapter number
    U]. Hick speaks throughout of "religious traditions."
- **James**, "The Will to Believe" (1896), in *The Will to Believe and Other Essays* (New York:
  Longmans, 1897), pp. 25–26 [V; Gutenberg #26659, page markers].
  - "What then do we now mean by the religious hypothesis? … religion says essentially two things":
    "the best things are the more eternal things," and "we are better off even now if we believe her
    first affirmation to be true."
  - James's hypothesis is deliberately generic and includes a practical clause.

## 4. Hawking and Davies

- **Hawking**, *A Brief History of Time* (New York/London: Bantam, 1988), last paragraph, **p. 175**
  in the first edition [V2]. Ferguson and others cite p. 175. The wording is checked against a Bantam 1989 export scan (archive.org), where it
  falls at p. ~185:
  - "If we find the answer to that, it would be the ultimate triumph of human reason—for then we
    would know the mind of God" [V wording].
  - The 1996/98 updated edition reads "the answer to that question" [V2; OL search].
- **Hawking's glosses.**
  - In *Black Holes and Baby Universes* (1993) he says he "nearly cut the last sentence in the book"
    at proof stage [V2; quoted in Flew, *Philosophical Essays*, 1998].
  - In 2014 (*El Mundo* interview): "we would know everything that God would know, if there were a
    God, which there isn't" [V2; Ferguson, *Stephen Hawking: His Life and Work*, 2016]. This is good
    material for the paper: even "knowing the mind of God" is offered as a counterfactual.
- **Davies**, *The Mind of God: The Scientific Basis for a Rational World* (New York: Simon &
  Schuster, 1992). The UK subtitle is *Science and the Search for Ultimate Meaning* [U].
  - Thesis: the universe's intelligibility and our capacity to grasp it point to meaning, not
    accident.
  - He also argues that explanation cannot close by reason alone [U chapter refs].
  - Last line, p. 232: "We are truly meant to be here" [V2; multiple citers give 1992: 232].
- **Responses.**
  - Mary Midgley, *Science as Salvation* (London: Routledge, 1992), which criticises Hawking's
    finale as scientistic quasi-religion [V2; Midgley and Appleyard noted as having "denounced
    Hawking"].
  - Wertheim, *Pythagoras' Trousers* (1995); Flew, *Philosophical Essays* (1998); van Huyssteen,
    *Duet or Duel?* (1998). All three discuss the passage [V].
  - Hawking and Mlodinow, *The Grand Design* (2010), Hawking's own later retreat [U quotation].

## Recommendations for the paper

1. **Atheism.** Adopt Draper's propositional sense: "the proposition that God does not exist." Note
   Flew's "simply not a theist" (negative atheism) once, as the psychological sense the paper is
   compatible with (spine §6.6).
2. **Agnosticism.** Distinguish three things:
   - Huxley's *method* (the CE V p. 246 quotation, which is the exact wording the spine uses);
   - Draper's *epistemological* sense, "neither theism nor atheism is known";
   - the paper's stronger thesis, which says such knowledge is unobtainable from experience.

   Call the paper's position "agnosticism in the epistemological sense, strengthened," and avoid
   "strong agnosticism," which Draper uses for a *rational-requirement* thesis. Cite Draper's
   agnostic-atheist and fideism symmetry in the objections section.
3. **Unit of analysis.** Use **"worldview"** (after Smart), defined to include secular naturalism.
   - Use "religious tradition" only for historical communities (Smith's "cumulative tradition").
   - Reason: the paper's claim covers naturalism, Smart's term explicitly does, and Smith's term
     names a historical deposit, not a truth-apt content.
   - James's "religious hypothesis" is the closest analogue of a "theory." Keep Hick's "the Real"
     for §5.
4. **Quotations to use.**
   - Hawking, p. 175 (title).
   - Hawking's 2014 counterfactual gloss (a footnote; mark it as an interview).
   - Huxley, CE V 246 and 310.
   - Kant B xxx (Kemp Smith). It is the obvious "Hume/Kant" foil for objection 5.
   - James, pp. 25–26.
   - Davies, p. 232, as the optimistic counterpoint.
