# Lit strand 02: Religious epistemology and externalism

Checked against SEP, Crossref, publishers and primary texts. **UNVERIFIED** = not confirmed from a source.

## 1. The §4 taxonomy against the standard surveys

**The surveys**

- **SEP "Epistemology of Religion"**, Peter Forrest (1997; rev. 26 Oct 2025).
  - Sections: Enlightenment Evidentialism rejected / defended; Natural Theology; Newman; Fideism; Religious Experience, Revelation and Tradition; Religious Disagreement.
  - No section on testimony or virtue epistemology.
- **SEP "Religious Experience"**, Mark Webb (rev. 2022).
  - Swinburne's credulity principle: "what seems to one to be present actually is present, unless some appropriate defeater is operative."
  - Alston's doxastic practices.
- **SEP "Faith"**, Bishop & McKaughan (rev. 2023).
  - Models: affective; knowledge (Plantinga's A/C); belief; trust; doxastic venture; "venturing faith, without belief"; hope; faith as a virtue.
- **SEP "Fideism"**, Amesbury (rev. 24 Sep 2026).
  - Faith *beyond* reason vs faith *against* reason (Evans); supra- vs counter-evidential (Bishop); Wittgensteinian fideism.
  - Lists Pascal, Kierkegaard and **James** among "the usual suspects".
- **Dougherty & Tweedt, "Religious Epistemology"**, *Philosophy Compass* 10(8) (2015): 547–559, doi:10.1111/phc3.12185. The master divide is "'fideists', 'Reformed epistemologists', and 'evidentialists'".
- **Fuqua, Greco & McNabb (eds.), *Cambridge Handbook of Religious Epistemology*** (CUP 2023), doi:10.1017/9781009047180.
  - Chapters include natural theology, evidence, Reformed epistemology, miracles, pragmatic arguments, fideism, testimony, disagreement, knowledge-first, disjunctivism, debunking, and five traditions.
  - I found no "Routledge Handbook of Religious Epistemology".
- **Abraham & Aquino (eds.), *Oxford Handbook of the Epistemology of Theology*** (OUP 2017), doi:10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199662241.001.0001. Separate chapters on revelation/scripture, testimony (Lackey), the inner witness of the Spirit, tradition, authority (Zagzebski), virtue (Baehr), and disagreement.

**Assessment**

- The survey categories map onto routes 1–5 and standards 1–4, so §4 holds up.
- No survey uses a routes × standards grid. That is the paper's own contribution.
- Eschatological verification is not a survey category.
- What §4 lacks or merges:
  1. **Disagreement** is treated as a cross-cutting defeater, not as a route or a standard.
  2. **Testimony, tradition and authority** are kept separate from **revelation**.
  3. **Miracles** are kept separate from natural theology.
  4. **Non-doxastic faith** (faith without belief, hope) is a category of its own.
  5. **The inner witness of the Spirit** is kept separate from the *sensus divinitatis*.
  6. **Virtue epistemology and knowledge-first** are kept separate from reliabilism.
  7. **Fideism's "beyond vs against reason"** cuts across the paper's split between routes 5 and 6.

## 2. Clifford vs James

**Clifford**

- "The Ethics of Belief", *Contemporary Review* 29 (Jan 1877), from p. 289. The end page is **UNVERIFIED**.
- Reprinted in *Lectures and Essays* (1879), vol. 2, pp. 177–211. Text: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Ethics_of_Belief
- §I, "The Duty of Inquiry":
  - Maxim: "To sum up: it is wrong always, everywhere, and for anyone, to believe anything upon insufficient evidence." The "it" is lowercase in the source.
  - Shipowner: "he had no right to believe on such evidence as was before him". He got his belief "not by honestly earning it in patient investigation, but by stifling his doubts."
- §III: "It is wrong in all cases to believe on insufficient evidence".

**James**

- "The Will to Believe", *New World* 5 (June 1896). The pages (327–347?) are **UNVERIFIED**.
- Reprinted in *The Will to Believe and Other Essays in Popular Philosophy* (1897), pp. 1–31 (Gutenberg #26659). Citations below are to the 1897 text.
- §I, p. 3: a genuine option is "forced, living, and momentous".
- §II, p. 7: "that delicious *enfant terrible* Clifford".
- §IV, p. 11, the thesis: "Our passional nature not only lawfully may, but must, decide an option between propositions, whenever it is a genuine option that cannot by its nature be decided on intellectual grounds". Leaving it open "is itself a passional decision".
- §VII, p. 17: "We must know the truth; and we must avoid error". These are "two separable laws".
- §X, pp. 25–26: "the best things are the more eternal things"; "we are better off even now if we believe her first affirmation to be true."
- §X, p. 28: "a rule of thinking which would absolutely prevent me from acknowledging certain kinds of truth if those kinds of truth were really there, would be an irrational rule."
- **Use.** James's condition "cannot by its nature be decided on intellectual grounds" is the paper's C′/C3. AA proves that the condition holds.

## 3. Externalism

**Plantinga, *Warranted Christian Belief*** (OUP 2000)

  - Ch. 6, "Warranted Belief in God", pp. 167–198, doi:10.1093/0195131932.003.0006. The A/C model is pp. 168–186.

## 4. KK, anti-luminosity, and the second-order claim

**Williamson and KK**

- Williamson, *Knowledge and Its Limits* (OUP 2000), doi:10.1093/019925656X.001.0001.
  - Ch. 4, "Anti-Luminosity" (pp. 93–113): "there are no non-trivial luminous conditions".
  - Ch. 5, "Margins and Iterations" (pp. 114–134): uses the argument "to refute the KK principle", via margins for error.
  - The verbatim definition (around p. 95) is **UNVERIFIED**.
- Greco, "Could KK Be OK?", *J. Phil.* 111 (2014): 169–197, doi:10.5840/jphil2014111411.
- Dorst, "Abominable KK Failures", *Mind* 128 (2019): 1227–1259, doi:10.1093/mind/fzy067.

**Externalist replies**

- Alston, "Level-Confusions in Epistemology", *Midwest Studies* 5 (1980): 135–150, doi:10.1111/j.1475-4975.1980.tb00401.x. Being justified differs from being justified in believing one is justified; this content was not checked against the text.
- Easy knowledge: Vogel, "Reliabilism Leveled", *J. Phil.* 97 (2000), doi:10.2307/2678454; Cohen, *PPR* 65 (2002): 309–329. On externalism, a reliably formed belief that one knows is itself knowledge. So in the good case the believer *may* know that they know.
- Sosa, *A Virtue Epistemology* (OUP 2007), doi:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199297023.001.0001: animal vs reflective knowledge.

**Assessment**

- Read literally, "no one can know that they know" is **false by the paper's own concessions**. Whoever grants good-case first-order externalist knowledge cannot exclude good-case second-order knowledge.
- KK failure does not help. It would make the claim trivial: true of everyone, about everything.
- What AA does support: no one can *establish*, without epistemic circularity, that they are in the good case rather than in a record-equivalent rival (C1/C2).
- Alston (no non-circular demonstration of reliability) and Plantinga (pp. 170, 499) grant this themselves.

## Recommendations for the paper

1. **Rephrase §0's third sense and §4B row 2:** "no one is in a position to establish, by any route and without epistemic circularity, that their source is reliable rather than one of its record-equivalent rivals." Optionally, in Sosa's terms: reflective knowledge of a worldview's truth is unavailable.
2. **Add a KK footnote.** The thesis does not depend on KK failure (Williamson) or on rejecting externalism. It concedes possible good-case first- and second-order knowledge. Cite Alston 1980 to show the distinction is the externalists' own.
3. **Cite Plantinga directly:**
   - p. 191 for de jure/de facto dependence;
   - pp. 170, 347 and 499 for his disclaimers;
   - p. 349 to show that parity objections reduce to the de facto question.
4. **Taxonomy:**
   - (a) Split route 2 into testimony/tradition/authority and revelation/inner witness.
   - (b) Note that non-doxastic faith is not belief, so it is consistent with the paper.
   - (c) Name disagreement as a cross-cutting defeater, linked to §5.
   - (d) Split §4B row 2 into reliabilism/proper function and virtue/knowledge-first; the AA answer is the same for both.
   - (e) Place routes 5 and 6 using the "beyond vs against reason" distinction.
   - (f) Present the grid as new against the survey baseline of evidentialism, Reformed epistemology and fideism.
5. **Quotation details:**
   - Clifford's lowercase "it";
   - the "very likely" wording is on p. 498, not in ch. 6;
   - leave the *New World* pagination and Clifford's end page flagged until checked.
