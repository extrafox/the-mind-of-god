## 1. Religious diversity

- **Hick, *An Interpretation of Religion*** (Macmillan 1989, DOI 10.1057/9780230371286; 2nd ed. 2004,
  same pagination). [V]
  - p. 12: "the universe is religiously ambiguous in that it is possible to interpret it,
    intellectually and experientially, both religiously and naturalistically."
  - p. 74: "It evokes and sustains non-religious as well as religious responses."
  - "The Religious Ambiguity of the Universe" is the running head on pp. 74–122. It is apparently
    the title of the part covering chs. 5–7, not the title of ch. 5. [part number U]
  - Ch. 14, "The Pluralistic Hypothesis", pp. 233–251. It sets "the Real an sich" against "the Real
    as variously experienced-and-thought" (p. 236), on a Kantian model.
  - The idea of ambiguity originates in *Faith and Knowledge*. Critics of the Real include
    Mavrodes, Plantinga, Rowe, and Netland. [V-sec: IEP "John Hick", Cramer]
- **Race**, *Christians and Religious Pluralism* (SCM, 1983). [V: reviewed in *Religious Studies* 20,
  1984, DOI 10.1017/s0034412500016474] That it originated the exclusivist/inclusivist/pluralist
  typology is only the usual attribution ("often said to have been inaugurated by Alan Race", Perry
  2006). Some credit D'Costa (1986) as well. [V-sec]
- **Plantinga, "Pluralism: A Defense of Religious Exclusivism"**, in Senor (ed.), *The Rationality of
  Belief and the Plurality of Faith* (Cornell, 1995), pp. 191–215. Note the full title. [V]
  - He rebuts the moral charges ("arrogant, or egotistical ... imperialistic, or oppressive", p. 195)
    and the epistemic ones ("Is the exclusivist irrational in these senses? I think not", p. 207).
- **Basinger**, *Religious Diversity: A Philosophical Assessment* (Ashgate, 2002). [V]
  - "Basinger's Rule": a truth-seeking exclusivist has a prima facie obligation to assess the rival
    claims. The label is used by others (Baker 2007); Basinger's own page is [U].
- **SEP "Religious Diversity (Pluralism)"**, Basinger. First published 2004, revised 21 Feb 2025. [V]
  - Exclusivists hold their view "is the truth or at least closer to the truth than any other";
    pluralists "deny the primacy of any one religion".
  - It distinguishes unitary pluralism (Hick) from radical pluralism (Heim).
- **Peer disagreement.** [V]
  - Feldman, "Reasonable Religious Disagreements", in Antony (ed.), *Philosophers without Gods*
    (OUP, 2007), 194–214, DOI 10.1093/oso/9780195173079.003.0016.
  - Kelly, "The Epistemic Significance of Disagreement", *Oxford Studies in Epistemology* 1 (2005),
    167–196.
  - Christensen, "Epistemology of Disagreement: The Good News", *Phil. Review* 116 (2007), 187–217,
    DOI 10.1215/00318108-2006-035.
  - King, "Disagreement: What's the Problem? or A Good Peer Is Hard to Find", *PPR* 85 (2012),
    249–272, DOI 10.1111/j.1933-1592.2010.00441.x.
  - King, "Religious Diversity and its Challenges to Religious Belief", *Phil. Compass* 3 (2008),
    830–853.
  - SEP "Disagreement", Frances and Matheson (revised 2024).

## 2. Eschatological verification

- **Hick, "Theology and Verification"**, *Theology Today* 17(1) (1960): 12–31, DOI
  10.1177/004057366001700104. [V]
  - **The parable.** Two travellers walk one road, one believing it leads to the Celestial City,
    the other to nowhere. They have the same experiences and read them differently, but "when they
    do turn the last corner it will be apparent that one of them has been right all the time and the
    other wrong." [V-sec: IEP quoting Hick; page U]
  - **The asymmetry.** Theism is verifiable if true and not falsifiable if false, since there would
    be no afterlife. So it is "a genuinely factual assertion" (*Faith and Knowledge*, 2nd ed., p.
    195). [V-sec]
- **Critics.**
  - Tooley, "John Hick and the Concept of Eschatological Verification", *Religious Studies* 12 (1976):
    177–199, DOI 10.1017/s003441250000915x. [V]
  - Nielsen, "Eschatological Verification", *Canadian Journal of Theology* 9 (1963): 271–281. [U]
  - For the defence: Badham, "Eschatological Verification Reconsidered", in *A John Hick Reader*
    (1990), 126–144, DOI 10.1057/9780230379800_8. [V]
- **Background: Flew, "Theology and Falsification"** (*University* symposium with Hare and Mitchell,
  1950–51). Reprinted in Flew and MacIntyre (eds.), *New Essays in Philosophical Theology* (SCM, 1955).
  It uses Wisdom's gardener parable ("Gods", 1944–45), and contains "death by a thousand
  qualifications". [U: pages and wording]

## 3. The evil-god challenge

- **Law, "The Evil-God Challenge"**, *Religious Studies* 46(3) (2010): 353–373, DOI
  10.1017/s0034412509990369. [V]
  - **The form.** An evil god is roughly as reasonable as a good one. The cosmological and design
    arguments support both. The "problem of good" mirrors the problem of evil, and each theodicy
    has a "reverse theodicy". So the theist owes an asymmetry. [standard summary; check the text]
- **Replies.** [V]
  - Weaver, "Evilism, Moral Rationalism, and Reasons Internalism", *IJPR* 77 (2015): 3–24, DOI
    10.1007/s11153-014-9472-3.
  - Collins, "The Evil-God Challenge: Extended and Defended", *Religious Studies* 55 (2019): 85–109,
    DOI 10.1017/s0034412518000070. This is pro-Law.
  - Collins, "The Privation Theory of Evil and the Evil-God Challenge", *Religious Studies* 62 (online
    2024): 121–139.
  - Hendricks, "Sceptical Theism and the Evil-God Challenge", *Religious Studies* 54 (2018): 549–561,
    DOI 10.1017/s0034412518000094.
  - Lancaster-Thomas, "The Evil-God Challenge Part I/II", *Phil. Compass* 13 (2018), DOIs
    10.1111/phc3.12502 and 10.1111/phc3.12543. These are the surveys.
  - Lancaster-Thomas, "Encountering Evil: The Evil-god Challenge from Religious Experience", *EJPR*
    12(3) (2020): 137–161. This bears on route 3.
- **Precursors.**
  - Cahn, "Cacodaemony", *Analysis* 37 (1977): 69–73, DOI 10.1093/analys/37.2.69. [V]
  - Madden and Hare, *Evil and the Concept of God* (1968). [V]
  - Millican, "The Devil's Advocate", *Cogito* (1989). [U]

## 4. Hiddenness and skeptical theism

- **Schellenberg.**
  - *Divine Hiddenness and Human Reason* (Cornell, 1993). [V-sec]
  - "The Hiddenness Argument Revisited" I and II, *Religious Studies* 41 (2005): 201–215 and 287–303.
    [V]
  - *The Hiddenness Argument* (OUP, 2015). [V]
- **The form** (SEP "Divine Hiddenness", Howard-Snyder and Green, revised 2022). [V]
  - 1993: there are people "capable of relating personally to God" who "through no fault of their
    own, fail to believe". If an unsurpassably great personal God exists, there are no such people.
    So there is no such God.
  - Later: perfect love entails openness to relationship. That entails no "nonresistant" nonbelief.
    Such nonbelief exists.
- **Replies** (SEP taxonomy). [V]
  - Greater goods: Murray, Swinburne, Moser.
  - Classical theism denies the premise about perfect love: Rea 2015.
  - Skeptical theism: McBrayer and Swenson 2012.
  - Relationship without belief: Alston, McKaughan.
  - Denying that nonresistant nonbelief exists.
- **Skeptical theism.** [V]
  - Wykstra, "The Humean Obstacle to Evidential Arguments from Suffering", *IJPR* 16 (1984): 73–93,
    DOI 10.1007/bf00136567.
  - CORNEA stands for "Condition On ReasoNable Epistemic Access". Wykstra gave several versions, and
    his exact wording is [U]. SEP's simplified form: believing *p* on *q* is reasonable only if one
    can reasonably believe that, were *p* false, *q* would probably differ.
  - Bergmann, *Noûs* 35 (2001): 278–296, DOI 10.1111/0029-4624.00297. ST1: "We have no good reason for
    thinking that the possible goods we know of are representative of the possible goods there are".
    ST2 says the same of evils, ST3 of the entailments between them.
  - SEP "Skeptical Theism", Perrine (2023).
- **The cost to knowability.** If God's reasons are inscrutable, divine deception about revelation
  cannot be excluded: Wielenberg, "Sceptical Theism and Divine Lies", *Religious Studies* 46 (2010):
  509–523, DOI 10.1017/s0034412510000247. Nor can genuine divine commands be sifted from purported
  ones (Maitzen 2007). [V]

## 5. The Great Pumpkin objection

- **Plantinga.**
  - "Is Belief in God Properly Basic?", *Noûs* 15 (1981): 41–51, DOI 10.2307/2215239. [V; page of the
    passage U]
  - "Reason and Belief in God", in *Faith and Rationality* (Notre Dame, 1983), pp. 16–93. [V]
  - **The form** (RBG p. 74): "If belief in God can be properly basic, why cannot just any belief be
    properly basic? ... What about the belief that the Great Pumpkin returns every Halloween?" [V via
    DeRose]
  - **The reply** (p. 78): there are relevant differences, since "God has implanted in us a natural
    tendency" to believe in him, and "the same cannot be said for the Great Pumpkin, there being no
    Great Pumpkin". The criteria for proper basicality are reached inductively. [V]
- **Son of Great Pumpkin.** Martin, *Atheism: A Philosophical Justification* (Temple, 1990). [pages U]
  As Plantinga states it in *Warranted Christian Belief* (OUP, 2000), ch. 10:
  - (1) "If Reformed epistemologists can legitimately claim that belief in God is rationally
    acceptable in the basic way, then for any other belief accepted in some community, the
    epistemologists of that community could legitimately claim that it was properly basic, no matter
    how bizarre the belief."
  - (2) The consequent is false.
  - (3) So they cannot legitimately make the claim.
  - Plantinga's verdict: "Son of Great Pumpkin does no better than Great Pumpkin" (p. 349; discussion
    from p. 346). [V via DeRose]
- **DeRose, "Voodoo Epistemology"** (comments to the Society of Christian Philosophers, 27 Dec 1999).
  https://campuspress.yale.edu/keithderose/voodoo-epistemology/ [V]
  - It separates the easy question from the hard question: "whether the Pumpkinites can use
    Plantinga's defense as well as Plantinga can."

## Recommendations for the paper

1. **§3/§5 (C3(a)).** Quote Hick 1989, p. 12. Mark the difference: Hick's ambiguity is
   *interpretive*; C3(a) is *extensional identity over every possible record*. The paper explains
   the ambiguity that Hick only observes. Cite ch. 14 for the Real.
2. **The typology (C′).** Cite Race as "usually credited" and use the SEP definitions.
   - Anticipate **Plantinga 1995** (the exclusivist is not irrational). The paper concurs (§0) and
     should say so.
   - Anticipate **peer disagreement** (Feldman, Kelly, Christensen, King) as a rival route to
     agnosticism. The paper's argument is structural and survives steadfast views. One paragraph is
     enough to establish novelty.
3. **§4B.5.** Give the parable faithfully. Reply: "turning the last corner" is one more finite
   experience that rival realities can produce (C1). No one can know the open phase has ended (C2,
   prefix form). Cite Tooley 1976 and Nielsen 1963 (verify first); Badham 1990 is the defender.
4. **The evil-god challenge (§5).** Cite Law 2010, the Lancaster-Thomas surveys, and Cahn 1977.
   - Anticipate Weaver 2015 (moral rationalism), Hendricks 2018 (skeptical theism), and Collins 2024
     (privation theory).
   - Claim only that cheap rivals are not strawmen, not exact symmetry. That sidesteps the asymmetry
     replies.
5. **Parity (§5).** Quote DeRose's "hard question" and Martin's form as given in WCB. Plantinga's
   reply makes the de jure question depend on the de facto one, which is exactly what §4B.2 uses:
   each community's claim is conditional on an unsettled de facto truth.
6. **§6.9.** Give Schellenberg's form from SEP. Hiddenness aims to refute God, which is stronger than
   the paper's claim. The paper's C1 holds even if belief were made available, so the paper does
   not depend on hiddenness.
   - Wielenberg and Maitzen show that skeptical theism undermines knowledge of revelation. That is
     convergent support; cite it.
   - Expect Bergmann's externalist reply, and answer it with §4B.2.
7. **Verify before drafting:** the *Noûs* 1981 page; Martin's pages; the range of WCB ch. 10;
   Nielsen 1963; Flew 1955; the page of the parable in Hick 1960; Basinger's own use of "Basinger's
   Rule"; Wykstra's exact wording of CORNEA.
