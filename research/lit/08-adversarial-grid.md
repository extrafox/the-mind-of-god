# 08. Adversarial review of the uniform grid (spine v1.1)

AA checked against `paper/sections/03`, `04`, `06`. [verify] marks citations still needing a page check. Most attacks exploit two facts: C1's proof uses only that a method's output depends on its input record (it is Cartesian), and the relativized theorem and ideal-agent concession depend on which worlds count as rivals.

## 1. Externalism and the testimony of the Spirit: SERIOUS

**Attack.** §4B row 2 says no one can rule out a reality where their source is unreliable, "so no one can know that they know". By externalist lights that inference is invalid.

- On Plantinga's extended A/C model (*Warranted Christian Belief*, 2000, chs. 8–9), the belief "my belief is warranted" can come from the same properly functioning process.
- Lemma L shows only that the belief "our world is in my success class" is "not supported by the record". The externalist agrees and denies that such support is needed.
- Safety theorists (Sosa 1999; Williamson 2000, ch. 5) say the rival worlds are remote.
- The objection does not even need KK failure. Sense 3 ("known to be known") is simply false for an externalist unless it is read internally at the second level. Read that way, it begs the question.

**Best reply.** Read sense 3 as *non-circular second-order certification*: one cannot show that one knows by a route that does not presuppose the first-order answer. That is C1 applied to "my source is reliable", and it binds every route.

Cite Vogel (2000) and Cohen (2002) on bootstrapping, and enlist Plantinga as an ally: "But is it true? … here we pass beyond the competence of philosophy" (WCB, closing pages [verify]).

**Minimal change.** Redefine sense 3 in §0. Row 2 then reads: "first-order knowledge possible; not non-circularly showable".

## 2. Acquaintance and perception: the argument proves too much. SERIOUS (fatal unless conceded)

**Attack.** The move "rival realities produce the same experience" applies word for word to vision.

C1 is indifferent to content (every visual record fits table-free computable worlds), and C3(a) is the brain in a vat. So the grid shows no one knows the external world exists, in any of the three senses.

This parity is old, and theists use it. Alston (1986, "Epistemic circularity"; 1991, ch. 3) argued *for* mystical perception on exactly these grounds: every doxastic practice is epistemically circular. Al-Ghazālī's *Munqidh* runs the same parity, with certainty restored as "a light God cast into the breast". As it stands, the thesis is either global scepticism or special pleading.

**Best reply (two tiers).**

*(a) Concede parity at the source level, using AA D7.*

- *Behaviour-level claims* are transiently underdetermined. Example: "there is a table", read as a claim about how the record continues. An ideal agent converges on these.
- *Source-level claims* are permanently underdetermined for every agent (C3(a)), and C1 binds them at every stage. Example: "God, matter, or a simulator produces the record".
- Perceptual claims keep their content under the behaviour reading; theism, naturalism and metaphysical realism do not. The target is Kantian agnosticism about the ground of experience: parity with a theory of everything, not with tables.

*(b) Locate what is specific to religion in C′, not in C1–C3.*

Perception is public, repeatable and cross-checked; religious experience across traditions does not converge on one source (Alston's hardest problem, 1991, ch. 7). Cross-checking does not defeat C1, but it narrows the near-tie field: a difference of degree that must be argued.

**Minimal change.**

- Add to §0: "These are parity results: they bind every claim about the ultimate source, including naturalism. What is specific to religion is the width of the surviving field."
- Add the objection "this is global scepticism".

## 3. The mapping: are worldviews models over a record? SERIOUS

**Attack.** In AA a model is a single computable function. Many doctrines are not about the record at all:

- the Trinity and divine simplicity have no record-extension;
- grace is a claim about a route, not about the record;
- only eschatology predicts.

For doctrines that say nothing about the record, C3(a) is a tautology. Aquinas already grants that the Trinity cannot be known by natural reason (ST I q.32 a.1).

Objection 4 (non-descriptive language) misses the main case. Classical theism holds these doctrines to be *descriptive but not predictive* (analogy; apophasis).

**Best reply.**

- Map a worldview to a *class of worlds* (a constraint), not to a single model.
- "Certify W" then means "certify that the actual world lies in K_W". C1 applies whenever the record does not entail W.
- Lemma L then gives a clean result: *holding a worldview is holding a bias.*
- Doctrines that say nothing about the record enter only through testimony, and testimony is answered in §1.

**Minimal change.**

Map a worldview to a class of models in §3; give objection 4 a third case, "descriptive but not predictive", admitting part of the answer is trivial.

## 4. Grace as an oracle (P2*): MINOR to SERIOUS

**Attack.** The relativized theorem needs rival worlds *at the oracle's level*. The theist excludes them by divine veracity: God "can neither deceive nor be deceived" (*Dei Filius* ch. 3). Then grace as a high oracle in a computable creation converges, by AA's own ideal-agent concession.

**Assessment.** The argument is conditional, not question-begging, and omnipotence even supplies the possibility of such rivals. What the theist relies on is a restriction to relevant alternatives, justified by God's goodness. That is Descartes' move, and the Cartesian circle comes with it. In Lemma L's terms, it is a bias restated as a belief about the world.

**Factual error.** AA claims the relativized C2 *for deterministic methods only*. Lot-casting combined with grace is therefore not covered.

**Minimal change.**

Make §2's C2 claim conditional and deterministic-only; concede that if the rivals are excluded, convergence is possible but cannot be shown (C1); add the divine-veracity reply.

## 5. Certitude versus certainty: SERIOUS (a misreading)

**Attack.** Aquinas says faith is more certain than *scientia* "simpliciter", by its *cause* (the First Truth), and less certain "secundum quid", relative to our intellect (ST II-II q.4 a.8). Certitude from the cause is objective and externalist, not psychological firmness.

Vatican I goes further. God "can be known with certainty" by natural reason (*Dei Filius* ch. 2, can. 2.1), and the motives of credibility are "most certain signs" (ch. 3). Calling all of this "psychological certitude" is a strawman.

**Best reply.**

Take the distinction from Newman (*Grammar of Assent*, ch. 7: certitude of the mind, certainty of propositions). File Thomist certitude under row 2; file Vatican I's natural certainty under row 1 and deny it by C1. Use Aquinas where he agrees: no one both believes and has *scientia* of the same thing (II-II q.1 a.5).

**Minimal change.** Rewrite the route-5 cell. In route 1, name *Dei Filius* ch. 2 as the claim the paper rejects.

## 6. Other attacks

**Tawātur: SERIOUS for the Islamic material.** Mass-transmitted reports are held to yield necessary knowledge (*ʿilm ḍarūrī*; al-Ghazālī, *al-Mustaṣfā* [verify]).

Reply: excluding collusion restricts the worlds; and tawātur certifies at most the *transmission*, not the divine source, which needs *iʿjāz* (route 1). Split route 2 into transmission and source.

**MINOR.** Lackey and anti-reductionism about testimony are externalist (§1 answers them). MacIntyre's epistemological crisis is comparative and fallibilist, so it belongs with C′ and T4 (cite as convergent). Newman's illative sense threatens P2, but C1 does not need P2.

**Contextualism (DeRose 1995): SERIOUS, and linked to §2.**

Raising C1's rivals raises the standard; in everyday contexts the believer "knows". §4B omits contextualism and knowledge-first epistemology, so "any view is some route held to some standard" overclaims. Add both, or drop the completeness claim.

**Precision error: SERIOUS.** The spine calls "madness, a deceiver, a simulation" C1 rivals. They are not.

C1's rivals agree *so far* and differ on the unobserved; rivals agreeing on the *whole* record fall under C3(a). Theism and naturalism are C1/C2 rivals if they differ eschatologically (Hick's limit then settles behaviour), C3(a) rivals if never. Each route cell must say which.

## Verdict

**(a) Does the uniform grid survive?** Only in a reduced form.

- Certification (C1) is uniform across all cells. But it is uniform *because* it is Cartesian, so it binds perception and naturalism equally, and must be stated as a parity.
- Under externalism the spine itself concedes first-order knowledge, so the uniform outcome is "not non-circularly certifiable", not "not known".
- With that restatement the grid is uniform. What remains new (C2 and C3 reaching oracle routes; the source/behaviour split; a worldview as a bias) is real, but smaller than the spine claims.

**(b) The single most important revision before drafting.** Recast the core claim as a *parity thesis at the source level*:

> No claim about the ultimate source of the record can be certified, reliably reached, or non-circularly known to be known, whether theistic, naturalistic or sceptical. Religious worldviews are placed no worse and no better than a theory of everything. What is specific to religion is the width of the surviving field (C′), and that some traditions claim more than parity permits (Vatican I, *tawātur*, *kashf* as *yaqīn*).

This revision defuses §2 and repairs §1 and §5.
