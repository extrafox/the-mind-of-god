## 1. Simplicity (Swinburne) and critics

**Swinburne**
- *The Existence of God*, 2nd ed. (OUP, 2004), doi:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199271672.001.0001; ch. 5,
  "The Intrinsic Probability of Theism", pp. 93–109.
  - OUP's abstract, which may not be his wording: theism "postulates one personal being, God,
    essentially omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly free and eternal".
  - The same abstract calls simplicity "the major a priori factor determining the prior probability".
- *Is There a God?* (OUP, 1996), ch. "The Simplicity of God", pp. 38–47.
- *Simplicity as Evidence of Truth* (Marquette UP, 1997). Content not checked.
- "A simple theism for a mixed world: response to Bradley", *Religious Studies* 43 (2007): 271–277,
  doi:10.1017/s0034412507009006. Theism versus an "Epicurean" rival.

**Critiques**
- **Gwiazda**, *Religious Studies* 45 (2009): 487–493, doi:10.1017/s0034412509990217.
  - Swinburne denies that any person can be both omniscient and perfectly free.
  - Resolving that undercuts "infinite properties are simple".
  - Swinburne's reply: *Philosophia* 39 (2011): 393–396.
- **Langtry**, "Swinburne on the Simplicity of Theism", *EJPR* 3(2) (2011): 409–426,
  doi:10.24204/ejpr.v3i2.403. "Postulating a property" is problematic; the limitless-power argument is
  weaker than claimed.
- **Draper**, "Simplicity and Natural Theology", in Bergmann & Brower (eds), *Reason and Faith* (OUP,
  2016), 48–63. Simplicity derives from probabilistic coherence, which "significantly weaken[s]" the
  cumulative case.
- **C. Miller**, *Religious Studies* 52 (2016): 45–61, doi:10.1017/s0034412514000523. Sympathetic
  survey and defence.
- **Wielenberg**, *Philosophia Christi* 11 (2009): 111–125. Dawkins's "God is complex" misses
  classical theism.
- **Oppy**, *Arguing about Gods* (CUP, 2006), and **Dawes**, *Theism and Explanation* (Routledge,
  2009): where each treats Swinburne's simplicity is UNVERIFIED.
- **Sober**, *Ockham's Razors* (CUP, 2015): the visible pages do not discuss theism.
- **Not found:** Kelly/Glymour-style or Goodman-style critiques applied to theism. Mackie and Gale are
  UNVERIFIED.

**Kolmogorov / description-language relativity**
- **No published use against Swinburne was found.** This is provisional, since PhilPapers and Scholar
  were not searched.
- Nearest: Dowe, Gardner & Oppy, "MML and Fine-Tuning" (unpublished, c. 2007; Figshare,
  doi:10.4225/03/56ea4b5d31658).
  - Uses Kolmogorov complexity against design and multiverse explanations of fine-tuning.
  - Notes that choosing a language "implicitly asserts a prior probability distribution".
  - Does not mention Swinburne, and treats language variance as manageable rather than as an
    objection.
- Background: "Bayes not Bust!", *BJPS* 58 (2007): 709–754, doi:10.1093/bjps/axm033.
- To check: S. Carroll, "Does the Universe Need God?" (Blackwell Companion to Science and
  Christianity, 2012), which matches "Kolmogorov complexity"; the context is UNVERIFIED.

## 2. Religious experience

- **Alston**, *Perceiving God* (Cornell UP, 1991). Doxastic practices; Christian mystical practice is
  rationally engaged in. Page numbers on diversity are UNVERIFIED.
- **Swinburne's Principle of Credulity**, SEP paraphrase: one is justified in believing "what seems to
  one to be present actually is present, unless some appropriate defeater is operative." His own
  wording and the Principle of Testimony are UNVERIFIED.
- **SEP "Religious Experience"** (Webb; revised 2022). Diversity and naturalistic explanation are "the
  two most important defeaters"; diversity is likened to "wildly conflicting eyewitness reports".
- **SEP "Mysticism"** (Jones & Gellman; revised 4 Sep 2026; cite both authors).
  - Sets out the "Argument from Experience".
  - §9.3.1: sense perception can "triangulate"; experience of the transcendent cannot.
  - §9.8: neuroscience is "neutral".
- **Otto**, *The Idea of the Holy*, trans. Harvey (OUP, 1923); *Das Heilige* (1917). The "mysterium
  tremendum" wording is UNVERIFIED from the text.
- **Proudfoot**, *Religious Experience* (University of California Press, 1985). SEP: ineffability is a
  rule for how the experience is described, not a description of it (pp. 125–27). The descriptive vs.
  explanatory reduction distinction is UNVERIFIED from the text.
- **Fales**, "Scientific Explanations of Mystical Experiences, Part I: The Case of St Teresa",
  *Religious Studies* 32 (1996): 143–163, doi:10.1017/s0034412500024203; Part II, 297–313,
  doi:10.1017/s0034412500024367. Cross-checkability is necessary for any perceptual practice.
- **Griffiths, Richards, McCann & Jesse**, *Psychopharmacology* 187 (2006): 268–283,
  doi:10.1007/s00213-006-0457-5, PMID 16826400. In a double-blind design, psilocybin occasioned
  mystical-type experiences that volunteers rated, at two months, as having "substantial personal
  meaning and spiritual significance".
- **Temporal-lobe epilepsy**
  - Dewhurst & Beard, *British Journal of Psychiatry* 117 (1970): 497–507,
    doi:10.1192/bjp.117.540.497.
  - Devinsky & Lai, *Epilepsy & Behavior* 12 (2008): 636–643, doi:10.1016/j.yebeh.2007.11.011.
    Ecstatic seizures are rare.
- **Persinger's "God helmet"** failed to replicate: Granqvist et al., *Neuroscience Letters* 379 (2005):
  1–6, doi:10.1016/j.neulet.2004.10.057. Effects were predicted "by suggestibility, not by the
  application of transcranial weak complex magnetic fields".

**Prayer trials**
- STEP: Benson et al., *American Heart Journal* 151 (2006): 934–942, doi:10.1016/j.ahj.2005.05.028,
  PMID 16569567.

    prayer vs 51% without.
  - Patients certain of prayer: 59% (RR 1.14).

  "equivocal". Whether it is current is UNVERIFIED.

  323:1450), retroactive prayer.

## 5. Berkeley

Quoted from Gutenberg eBooks #4723 and #4724, with editorial capitals normalised. Page numbers in
Luce & Jessop, *Works* II are UNVERIFIED.

- **PHK §3:** "Their *esse* is *percipi*".
- **PHK §29:** "the ideas imprinted on them are not creatures of my will. There is therefore some
  other Will or Spirit that produces them."
- **PHK §30:** "The set rules or established methods wherein the Mind we depend on excites in us the
  ideas of sense, are called the laws of nature".
- **PHK §147:** "God is known as certainly and immediately as any other mind or spirit whatsoever
  distinct from ourselves."
- **PHK §149:** God is "a Spirit who is intimately present to our minds, producing in them all that
  variety of ideas or sensations which continually affect us".
- **Second Dialogue:** "there must be some other mind wherein they exist. As sure, therefore, as the
  sensible world really exists, so sure is there an infinite omnipresent Spirit".
- **Third Dialogue:** "There is therefore some other Mind wherein they exist, during the intervals
  between the times of my perceiving them".
- **SEP "George Berkeley"** (Downing & Hilbert; revised 12 Aug 2026), §3.1.3.
  - Describes an argument "by elimination": ideas are passive, sensory ideas are involuntary, so their
    cause is "some other spirit".
  - It flags the hidden assumption that all mental causing is conscious willing.
