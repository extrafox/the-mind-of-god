# 06 — Novelty check

**Scope.** Prior work applying underdetermination, formal learning theory, or algorithmic information to theism, plus formal agnosticism arguments, "algorithmic agnosticism", "mind of God" with computation, and skeptical scenarios against religious experience.

**Method and limits.** Searched Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, Crossref and arXiv on 2026-11-01. Every item was checked against a metadata record (DOI or URL); abstracts were read where available. No general web search was available, and PhilPapers returned 403. "Not found" means not found in these indexes; do a manual PhilPapers or Google Scholar pass before submission.

---

## 1. Closest precedents (ranked)

**1. Keith DeRose, "Delusions of Knowledge Concerning God's Existence".** In Benton, Hawthorne and Rabinowitz (eds), *Knowledge, Belief, and God* (OUP 2018). doi:10.1093/oso/9780198798705.003.0015.
- *Argues:* "hardly anyone, if anyone at all, knows whether God exists". He takes religious experience as the most promising route and argues that, even where theists are right, it fails to give them knowledge.
- *Overlap:* closest in conclusion to our §0: knowledge-level agnosticism that concedes truth is possible.
- *Difference:* informal and one route only; no underdetermination theorem, convergence result, second-order (KK) analysis or full grid. *Must cite* as the foil for §4A route 3 and §4B standard 2.

**2. J. L. Schellenberg, *The Wisdom to Doubt: A Justification of Religious Skepticism*.** Cornell UP 2007; doi:10.7591/9780801462399. Reviews: Draper, *JAAR* 2008, doi:10.1093/jaarel/lfn064; Morriston, *IJPR* 2009, doi:10.1007/s11153-009-9220-2.
- *Argues* (summary from general knowledge, as no abstract was retrieved): neither belief nor disbelief in detailed religious claims is justified, given the immaturity of human inquiry and unconceived alternatives.
- *Overlap:* very high in conclusion and scope.
- *Difference:* the grounds are evolutionary-temporal ("we are early"), which is close to *transient* underdetermination. Ours is a *permanent*, in-principle limit. State the contrast explicitly.

**3. Peter Harrison, "Underdetermination and unconceived alternatives in science and theology: some historical perspectives".** *Religious Studies* 2025; doi:10.1017/s0034412525101029.
- *Argues:* extends Stanford's problem of unconceived alternatives. Medieval and early-modern thinkers explicitly used divine omnipotence ("God could have done it otherwise") to undermine realism.
- *Overlap and difference:* the only recent item joining "underdetermination" and "theology" in a philosophy-of-religion venue, but historical, and its underdetermination runs *from* theology *to* science, the reverse of ours. Shows *Religious Studies* is receptive.

**4. Hick and McKim on religious ambiguity.**
- John Hick, *An Interpretation of Religion* (1989; 2nd ed. 2004), doi:10.2307/1399358.
- Robert McKim, *Religious Ambiguity and Religious Diversity* (OUP 2001), doi:10.1093/0195128354.001.0001.
- *Argues:* the universe is religiously ambiguous, interpretable theistically or naturalistically.
- *Overlap and difference:* our C3(a)/§5 thesis in informal form, asserted from the shape of the evidence rather than derived from P1 and P2; no theorem, convergence result or grid.

**5. Swinburne-simplicity critics** (precedents for T3).
- Bruce Langtry, "Swinburne on the Simplicity of Theism", *EJPR* 2011, doi:10.24204/ejpr.v3i2.403. Argues that Swinburne's account of simplicity fails and that the argument from limitless power is weak.
- Calum Miller, "Is theism a simple hypothesis? The simplicity of omni-properties", *RS* 2015, doi:10.1017/s0034412514000523. A partial *defence*: omni-properties are simpler than "mega-property" parodies.
- Philippe Gagnon, "An Improbable God Between Simplicity and Complexity", *IPQ* 2013, doi:10.5840/ipq201353442. Replies to Dawkins on God's complexity; information theory used informally.
- Also: Herman Philipse, "Swinburne's apologetic strategy for theism evaluated", *RS* 2017, doi:10.1017/s0034412517000245.
- *Difference:* none of these abstracts invokes Kolmogorov complexity, the invariance theorem, or language-relativity of simplicity as a formal result. The machine-relativity reply appears to be new in this literature.

**6. Algorithmic information applied to God, outside the journals.** Two items found.
- Eliezer Yudkowsky, "Occam's Razor", LessWrong, 26 Sep 2007. Uses Solomonoff induction to argue that mind-involving hypotheses (Thor) are far more complex than Maxwell's equations. God appears only in the comments.
- Serkan Zorba, "God is Random: A Novel Argument for the Existence of God" (2016, PhilArchive ZORGIR). Uses Kolmogorov–Chaitin complexity and uncomputability to argue *for* God from quantum randomness.
- Both treat algorithmic information as deciding the question, one each way; ours argues it cannot (T3, C′). Cite Yudkowsky as the popular "theism is K-complex" form; Zorba is optional.

**7. Formal and Gödelian limits on knowledge of God.**
- Patrick Grim, *Noûs* 1988, doi:10.2307/2215708; Plantinga–Grim exchange, *Phil. Studies* 1993, doi:10.1007/bf00989730. Gödel/Cantor arguments that *omniscience* is impossible.
- Denys Turner, "Gödel, Thomas Aquinas, and the Unknowability of God", in *Kurt Gödel and the Foundations of Mathematics* (CUP 2011), doi:10.1017/CBO9780511974236.015.
- Driessen and Suarez (eds), *Mathematical Undecidability, Quantum Nonlocality and the Question of the Existence of God* (Kluwer 1997), doi:10.1007/978-94-011-5428-4.
- *Difference:* these concern God's nature or omniscience, or use incompleteness as analogy; none is a learning-theoretic limit on the *human inquirer*. Supports our §0 "not claimed" list and objection 7.

## 2. Other relevant items

**Agnosticism (definitions and defences)**
- Robin Le Poidevin, *Agnosticism: A Very Short Introduction* (OUP 2010), doi:10.1093/actrade/9780199575268.001.0001. It has chapters "Is agnosticism necessary?" and "Why be agnostic?". Use it for terms in §1.
- Anthony Kenny, "Worshipping an Unknown God", *Ratio* 2006, doi:10.1111/j.1467-9329.2006.00339.x; *The Unknown God: Agnostic Essays* (2004), reviewed in *Mind* 2006, doi:10.1093/mind/fzl421.
- Francis Jonbäck, "Default Agnosticism", *Religions* 2021, doi:10.3390/rel12010054. Uses a Plantingian strategy to argue that agnosticism is rationally acceptable as a default stance.
- Jane Friedman, "Rational Agnosticism and Degrees of Belief", *OSE* 4 (2013), doi:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199672707.003.0003. Suspension versus credence 0.5 (§4B standard 3).

**Theism versus naturalism, and parity**
- Randal Rauser, "Theism, Naturalism, and Invisible Gardeners", *Think* 2020, doi:10.1017/S1477175620000202. Argues naturalism is as adjustable as theism; a parity point for §5.
- Graham Oppy, *Arguing about Gods* (CUP 2006), doi:10.1017/cbo9780511498978. Argues (from general knowledge) that no argument about God's existence should persuade every reasonable person; near C′.
- Stephen Law, "The evil-god challenge", *RS* 2010, doi:10.1017/s0034412509990369. Already on the spine.

**Skeptical scenarios**
- Ian Huyett, "Religious Parallels to the Simulation Hypothesis: Gnosticism, Mormonism, and Neoplatonism", *Sophia* 2023, doi:10.1007/s11841-023-00955-2. Title only (no abstract retrieved); appears comparative.
- Jan Forsman, "Teresa's Demons", 2023, doi:10.1163/2666318x-bja00013. Teresa of Ávila's influence on Descartes's demon: a historical link between religious experience and the deceiver.
- Ian Wilks, "The Global Skepticism Objection to Skeptical Theism" (2013), doi:10.1002/9781118608005.ch31. Relevant to objection 9.
- No paper found that uses the simulation or brain-in-a-vat scenario specifically to defeat *knowledge* from religious experience. DeRose (item 1) is the nearest.

**Hawking and the "mind of God"**
- Marcus Hutter, "A Complete Theory of Everything (Will Be Subjective)", *Algorithms* 2010, doi:10.3390/a3040329; arXiv:0912.5434. Kolmogorov and observer-relative theories of everything.
- No item found that joins Hawking's "mind of God" to computability or underdetermination.

## 4. Verdict and suggested "Contributions" wording

**Verdict.** The *conclusion* is not new: DeRose 2018 (knowledge), Schellenberg 2007 (skepticism across traditions), Hick and McKim (ambiguity) and Oppy (argument) all hold versions of it. Cite them all; do not claim to originate religious agnosticism.

What appears **new**, with no prior work found doing any of it:
- deriving the conclusion from formal learning-theoretic and algorithmic-information results (C1–C3);
- the second-order "cannot know that one knows" result against externalism (C2, lemma L);
- applying machine-relative simplicity to the Swinburne debate;
- closing the "oracle" escape (P2\*);
- the route × standard grid with its uniform outcome.

**Positioning to state explicitly:**
- against Schellenberg: permanent, in-principle limits, not "we are early";
- against DeRose: every route, not religious experience alone, plus a second-order result;
- against Hick and McKim: ambiguity derived from P1 and P2, not observed in the evidence;
- against Yudkowsky and Swinburne: simplicity decides nothing, because it is machine-relative.

**Suggested wording:**

> *Contributions.* Agnosticism about God, and the religious ambiguity of the world, have been defended before (Hick 1989; McKim 2001; Schellenberg 2007; DeRose 2018). This paper gives the first derivation of that position from formal results in learning theory and algorithmic information. (1) We show that no route to religious belief — inference, testimony, experience, basic belief, faith, or practical choice — can certify a worldview, and that no followable route is guaranteed to converge on the true one; this holds for supernatural "oracle" routes too, with a precisely stated exception. (2) We show that externalist accounts of religious knowledge, while not excluded at the first order, cannot deliver knowledge that one knows. (3) We show that appeals to the simplicity of theism (or of naturalism) are relative to a choice of descriptive language and cannot break near-ties. (4) We organize the result as a grid of routes and standards of knowledge whose verdict is uniform, so that it extends to positions not discussed here, including secular naturalism.

(Soften "first derivation" to "to our knowledge" unless a manual PhilPapers search confirms it.)
