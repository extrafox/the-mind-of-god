# 09. First-person revelation (theophany, prophetic call) and the traditions' own tests

**Scope.** The *originating* encounter, as distinct from mystical union (route 3) and from
testimony (route 2b). [V] means checked this pass; [U] means unverified. Bible quotations are
NRSVue, checked on Bible Gateway.

## 1. Primary accounts

**Moses (Exod 3–4) [V].**
- Saw: "the angel of the Lord appeared to him in a flame of fire out of a bush" (3:2).
- Heard: "I am who I am… 'I am has sent me to you'" (3:14; note: "I will be what I will be").
- Doubt: "Who am I…?" (3:11); "they may not believe me… but say, 'The Lord did not appear to
  you'" (4:1).
- Signs: staff, hand, Nile water (4:2–9), "so that they may believe" (4:5), graded in case the
  first fails (4:8). They authenticate the *recipient to others*, not the source to Moses.

**Isaiah 6 [V].**
- Saw: "I saw the Lord sitting on a throne" (6:1). Response: "Woe is me! I am lost" (6:5); "Here
  am I; send me!" (6:8). He asks for no sign.

**Paul [V].** The three Acts accounts disagree:

| | Companions hear | Companions see | Who falls | Commission |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acts 9:3–9 | "heard the voice but saw no one" (9:7) | no one | Saul | given in Damascus (Ananias, 9:10–17, not quoted) |
| Acts 22:6–11 | "did not hear the voice" (22:9) | "saw the light" | Saul | given in Damascus (Ananias, 22:12–16, not quoted) |
| Acts 26:13–18 | not stated | the light shone around all | "we had all fallen" (26:14) | given directly by Jesus on the road (26:16–18) |

- Gal 1:11–12: "not of human origin… I received it through a revelation of Jesus Christ"; "I did
  not confer with any human" (1:16). 1 Cor 15:8: "as to one untimely born". Galatians insists on
  an unmediated source; Acts 9 and 22 route the commission through a human.

**Muhammad: Sahih al-Bukhari 3, Book 1 (Revelation) [V].**
- sunnah.com returned 403; checked against the Muhsin Khan translation (sunnah.com's) via the
  fawazahmed0 hadith-api mirror.
- "I do not know how to read"; pressed three times; Q 96:1–3. He returns "with his heart beating
  severely": "Cover me!"; "I fear that something may happen to me."
- Khadija: "Never! By Allah, Allah will never disgrace you", on moral grounds (kinship, the poor,
  guests). Waraqa: "This is the same one who keeps the secrets (angel Gabriel) whom Allah had
  sent to Moses."
- Authentication comes afterwards, from others, by character and by analogy with an earlier
  tradition.

**Joseph Smith's First Vision [V].** Sources are josephsmithpapers.org (the "Accounts of the First
Vision" index and the page transcripts) and the Gospel Topics essay "First Vision Accounts"
(churchofjesuschrist.org).
- **c. 1832 History, pp. 1–3** (only account in JS's hand): "in the 16th year of my age"
  (inserted by F. G. Williams); "a piller of fire light"; "I saw the Lord"; "thy sins are forgiven
  thee"; "I am the Lord of glory". One personage, no darkness.
- **1835 Journal, pp. 23–24** (scribe Warren Parrish): "my toung seemed to be swolen"; "a noise
  behind me"; "a personage appeard"; "another personage soon appeard like unto the first"; "<and I
  saw many angels in this vision>" (insertion); "about 14. years old".
- **1838 History, vol. A-1 = JS—History 1:14–20**: a power binds his tongue, "Thick darkness
  gathered around me" (v. 15); "a pillar of light… above the brightness of the sun" (v. 16); "two
  Personages… This is My Beloved Son. Hear Him!" (v. 17); "join none of them" (v. 19); "I have
  learned for myself" (v. 20).
- **1842 Wentworth letter**, *Times and Seasons*, pp. 706–707 (JS's role "not known"; "borrowed
  language from Orson Pratt"): "about fourteen"; "two glorious personages who exactly resembled
  each other"; "all religious denominations were believing in incorrect doctrines". No darkness.
- **Differences:** beings (one; two plus angels; two; two); motive (forgiveness in 1832, which
  church from 1835); age (15 vs 14); darkness (1835 as opposition, fully in 1838). The Church's
  essay cites audience, dictation and "growing insight", and concedes history cannot prove or
  disprove the vision.
- **JS—H 1:15–16** builds a *rival source* into the account: an "enemy" overpowered by the light.
  The account carries its own deceiver-hypothesis and its own test.

**A revelation later judged false: the "Satanic verses" [V for the Qur'an; partly U for the
historiography].**
- Q 53:19–20 [V, quran.com, Sahih Intl.]: "have you considered al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā? And Manāt…".
  Interpolation (Tabari): "These are the high-flying cranes and their intercession is to be hoped
  for" [U, via Hawting/Wikipedia]. Q 22:52 [V]: Satan "threw into" every prophet's recitation,
  "But Allāh abolishes that which Satan throws in."
- Source: al-Ṭabarī, *Taʾrīkh*, Ibn Isḥāq chain; *The History of al-Ṭabarī*, vol. 6, *Muḥammad at
  Mecca*, trans. W. M. Watt and M. V. McDonald (SUNY, 1988; ISBN 9780887067075) [V]; pages
  (usually pp. 107–112) [U]. Later rejection on *ʿiṣma* and *isnād* grounds (Ibn Khuzayma via
  al-Rāzī; Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ) [U, secondary]. Shahab Ahmed, *Before Orthodoxy* (Harvard UP, 2017;
  doi:10.4159/9780674977372) [V]: early *tafsīr*/*sīra* largely accepted it; rejection came later.
- Scripture itself concedes a satanic insertion *indistinguishable at reception*, corrected only
  afterwards.

## 2. The traditions' own discernment tests

- **Deut 13:1–3 [V].** Even if a prophet's "omens or portents" come true, "you must not heed" one
  who urges other gods. This is a **content** test that overrides fulfilment.
- **Deut 18:21–22 [V].** "How can we recognize a word that the Lord has not spoken?" The answer: if
  "the thing does not take place or prove true". This is a **prediction** test, and it is decisive
  only on failure.
- **1 John 4:1–2 [V].** "test the spirits to see whether they are from God". The criterion is a
  confession that "Jesus Christ has come in the flesh". This is a doctrinal test.
- **2 Cor 11:14 [V].** "Even Satan disguises himself as an angel of light". This states the
  whole-record rival in Scripture's own words.
- **Ignatius, *Spiritual Exercises*, Rules for the Second Week [V, CCEL, Mullan translation].**
  Rule 2: "It belongs to God our Lord to give consolation to the soul without preceding cause."
  Rule 4: the evil angel takes "the appearance of an angel of light", entering "with the devout
  soul" and going "out with himself". Rule 5: if "the beginning, middle and end is all good… it is
  a sign of the good Angel". A *trajectory* test: one moment cannot discriminate.
- **John of the Cross, *Ascent of Mount Carmel* II.16 [V, CCEL].**
  "both God and the devil can represent the same images and species"; so "it is well always to
  reject them".
- **Teresa of Ávila, *Life*, ch. 25 [V, Gutenberg, Lewis translation].**
  The divine locution "is a work done"; satanic ones leave "great aridity" and "disquiet", yet "the
  words of Satan are good"; slight emotions "are not a test". *Interior Castle* VI.3 parallel [U].
- **Islamic criteria [V for the texts quoted].**
  Q 81:25: "it is not the word of a devil, expelled". Q 26:221–222: devils "descend upon every
  sinful liar" (a **character** test, Khadija's). Q 114:4: "the retreating whisperer". Bukhari 6984
  [V, Khan]: "A true good dream is from Allah, and a bad dream is from Satan." The grading of
  *waḥy* / *ilhām* / *waswasa* (e.g. al-Ghazālī, *Iḥyāʾ* Book 21 on *khawāṭir*) is **[U]**.
- **LDS [V, churchofjesuschrist.org].**
  D&C 9:8–9 (to Oliver Cowdery, April 1829): "study it out in your mind; then you must ask me if
  it be right"; if right, a burning in the bosom; if not, "a stupor of thought". Moroni 10:4–5:
  "ask God… if these things are not true"; "by the power of the Holy Ghost ye may know the truth
  of all things". The faculty under test certifies itself.

## 3. Philosophical treatments

- **Swinburne, *Revelation: From Metaphor to Analogy*** (Oxford: Clarendon, 1992; 2nd ed. 2007;
  doi:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199212460.001.0001) [V on Crossref and OpenLibrary].
  Per the SEP: credible revelation needs important, not-improbable content; a violation miracle;
  an interpreting church; and plausible interpretations. Joint satisfaction "is an extremely
  unlikely event in the normal course of things" (2007: 337). Review: E. Stump, *Phil. Review*
  103(4) (1994): 739, doi:10.2307/2186116.
- **Mavrodes, *Revelation in Religious Belief*** (Philadelphia: Temple UP, 1988; ISBN 0877225451)
  [V]. Schema: "*m* reveals *a* to *n* by means of *k*" (p. 88, per the SEP). Three models:
  causation, manifestation, communication.
- **Wolterstorff, *Divine Discourse: Philosophical Reflections on the Claim That God Speaks***
  (Cambridge UP, 1995; doi:10.1017/CBO9780511598074) [V].
  Divine *speech* (by appropriation or authorization) versus manifestational revelation.
- **William J. Abraham** [V via the SEP bibliography]:
  *Divine Revelation and the Limits of Historical Criticism* (OUP, 1982); *Crossing the Threshold
  of Divine Revelation* (Eerdmans, 2006). Revelation is "polymorphous"; recognition needs an
  *oculus contemplationis*, miracles only corroborating.
- **SEP, "Divine Revelation"**, Mats Wahlberg (first published 2020; revised 26 Jun 2024) [V].
  §2.2.1 perceptual models; notes Pannenberg's charge of fideism against Barth's
  self-authentication.
- **Alston, *Perceiving God*** (Cornell UP, 1991) [V on OpenLibrary].
  Applied to theophany: first-order justification within a practice with an "overrider system";
  reliability not showable without epistemic circularity.
- **Circularity.** Every "self-authenticating" test above uses the channel under test to certify
  itself: the burning bosom, Teresa's "work done", Ignatius's consolation "without cause", and the
  light defeating the darkness.

## 4. Recommendations for the paper

1. **Finite record (P1).**
   - Each encounter enters as a finite, often redacted report; the JS accounts and Acts 9/22/26
     show even the *recipient's own* record varies.
   - Use in §4A, *not* to impugn sincerity; cite the LDS essay's concession.
2. **Whole-record rivals (C3(a)).**
   - The traditions themselves name the deceiver that fits the whole record: 2 Cor 11:14; Ignatius
     rule 4; John of the Cross ("both God and the devil can represent the same images"); Teresa
     ("the words of Satan are good"); Q 22:52; JS—H 1:15.
   - Muhammad's "I fear that something may happen to me" is the recipient entertaining the
     madness or *jinn* rival himself.
   - So C3(a) rivals are native to the traditions, not philosophers' inventions.
3. **The Satanic-verses case.**
   - Strongest exhibit: a reported reception misattributed, corrected only afterwards.
   - Present it as contested (Ahmed 2017); Q 22:52 alone states the possibility.
4. **P2\* oracle.**
   - Theophany is the paradigm non-effective channel. Grant it non-effective powers, and C1 and
     C3 still bind it.
   - Exod 4:1–9 shows that even the tradition requires signs for third parties. That moves
     authentication back into the public, finite record (route 1 or 2b).
5. **Sense-3 circularity.**
   - D&C 9:8, Moroni 10:4–5, Ignatius rule 2 and Teresa's "work done" are all *self-certifying*.
   - Pair them with Alston's concession and the SEP's Barth/Pannenberg note.
6. **The traditions' tests as imposed constraints (C′).** Classify each test:
   - **Prediction** (Deut 18:22): falsifies only (K1/Vulcan, as in prophecy, route 2b).
   - **Content** (Deut 13; 1 John 4:2; Q 81:25): presupposes the worldview, which is lemma L.
   - **Character** (Q 26:222; Khadija): this is evidence about the human recipient, not the source.
   - **Fruits or trajectory** (Ignatius rule 5; Teresa): this is behaviour, which can be settled
     (T4), not the source.
   - **Authority** (confessor, church): an imposed choice.

   None reaches the source. The traditions' tests narrow the field (C′) and leave the whole-record
   rival standing.
7. **Cut or verify before use:**
   - the al-Ghazālī *khawāṭir* claim [U];
   - the Tabari SUNY page range [U];
   - *Interior Castle* VI.3 [U].
