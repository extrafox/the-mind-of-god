# Referee report: "Knowing the Mind of God" (IJPR submission)

Clear and well organised, with more primary-source engagement than most such work. But the parity thesis rests on a phenomenalist reading of perception, the second-order result is Alston's (as the paper itself cites), and the formal import mostly relabels underdetermination built in by definition. Paths are relative to `paper/sections/`.

## 1. Parity and global scepticism

**1a. FATAL. The behaviour/source split is unstable** (01:49-51, 02:99-104, 07:10-18). Ordinary realism holds that *there is a mind-independent table causing my experience*. That is a claim about the source. The paper admits that "metaphysical realism" loses its claim to knowledge (07:17-18). So it accepts external-world scepticism and then redefines "there is a table" as a forecast about how experience will go on. That is phenomenalism, with the Chisholm/Sellars objections. It attacks Berkeley (07:86-96) while adopting his view of perception. It avoids *saying* "global scepticism" only by renaming it. **Fix:** say openly that the thesis is scepticism about source-level claims for every realist, and defend that position. Acknowledge that behaviour versus source is van Fraassen's empirical adequacy versus truth. Or explain why realism about tables survives and theism does not.

**1b. SERIOUS. The "bite" on religion is not a result** (01:37-39, 06:62-79). Once source-level claims are off the table for everyone, all that is left that is specific to religion is the "width of the field". That empirical point about diversity is already in Hick, McKim and Alston ch. 7, and AA adds nothing to it. **Fix:** say so, or derive the width formally.

**1c. SERIOUS. The paper treats proximate and ultimate source as the same level** (01:21-22, 03:13-19, 07:13-15). On classical theism, God is the primary cause *of* matter and law, so the two are not rival answers at one level, and a God working through a simulation is no rival to theism. **Fix:** engage primary and secondary causation (Aquinas *SCG* III.70, Kathryn Tanner, Feser) and recast the rival classes.

**1d. SERIOUS. "Whole-record rivals" are misclassified** (03:65-67, 04:101-107). Drugs, madness and seizures yield the same *episode*, not the same *whole record*. Toxicology, history and others' testimony separate them, so "nothing the experiencer could ever undergo separates them" is false. **Fix:** keep only deceiver and simulation as whole-record rivals. The rest differ on the unobserved.

## 2. Representation of traditions

**2a. SERIOUS. The paper misreads Vatican I and equivocates on "certainty"** (04:14-27, 05:15-24). Dei Filius's "certainty" is not entailment from a finite sensory record. Thomist demonstrations proceed *quia*, through principles held to be necessary, so "Canon 2.1 asserts precisely what the first result denies" equivocates. The paper is also silent on modality. If God exists necessarily, the "worlds outside the theistic class" (04:22-23) are impossible, and the first result begs the question against classical theism and the ontological argument (brushed aside at 04:41-43). CCC §36 quotes Dei Filius verbatim, so it is not "more cautious". **Fix:** define certification without trading on the conciliar term, argue for the modal premise, and engage Kerr, Feser and Oppy.

**2b. SERIOUS. The paper prescribes what faith is** (04:199-204, 07:27-32, 08:10-11). "Faith... understood as what it is: a choice" contradicts Dei Filius ch. 3 and canon 3.5 (faith "is not a blind movement of the mind"). It also contradicts Calvin, who defines faith as "a firm and certain knowledge" (*Inst.* III.2.7), and Aquinas II-II q.2. None of these is engaged. **Fix:** say "on our account" and engage those definitions.

**2c. SERIOUS. "The traditions themselves concede" a deceiver** (abstract 33-34, 04:130-141). This is selective. John of the Cross holds that the devil cannot produce "substantial words" (*Ascent* II.31). Ignatius holds that "consolation without preceding cause" comes from God alone (*SpEx* 330, 336). Teresa holds that the devil cannot counterfeit the effects of a true vision. The Islamic doctrine of *ʿiṣma* is the reading of 22:52 that the tradition itself gives. The traditions grant counterfeiting for some experiences and deny it for others. **Fix:** engage the non-counterfeitability claims, which are the real target.

**2d. MINOR. Quotations to correct or verify.** Calvin I.3.1 has "mind", not "minds" (04:164). "Burning in the bosom" is a paraphrase of D&C 9:8 "your bosom shall burn" (04:157). Vatican I ch. 3 (04:69-70) is paraphrased inside quotation marks. *Kuzari* I.8 precedes the Rabbi's entry (04:54). Check Hick p. 12 (likely around p. 73; 03:69, 06:4), *Ascent* II.16 and *Life* ch. 25 (04:134-135). Note the Acts 9:7/22:9 harmonisation.

**2e. MINOR. Overclaimed novelty on iʿjāz** (04:236). Kalam treatments of iʿjāz (al-Bāqillānī, ʿAbd al-Jabbār) already route "the challenge has not been met" through *tawātur*, and Vasalou 2002 probably discusses this. Delete the claim. Cite Weiss and Hallaq on *tawātur*.

**2f. MINOR. Thin and placeholder coverage.** "Orthodox" (abstract) is represented only by Dionysius. Add Palamas on the essence/energies distinction, and Lossky. Maimonides's *Guide* (II.1 demonstration, negative theology) is ignored. The Mīmāṃsā footnote (05:131-133) is a placeholder and misses Kumārila's point that the authorless Veda has no speaker-defects. Remove the "% [verify]" comments (06:71, 08:35).

## 3. The second-order claim

**3a. SERIOUS. Trivial and wrongly claimed as new** (01:73-74, 05:40-50, 07:70). The paper itself says that Alston proved epistemic circularity for *every* basic practice, perception included (05:48-50). So "a second-order result against externalism" is neither new nor against externalism, which accepts it (Alston 1986; Bergmann 2006). **Fix:** present it as Alston's point, generalised.

**3b. SERIOUS. The paper conflates two notions of reliability** (03:46-47, 05:42-45). The second result shows that no route succeeds *in every world*. Externalists need actual-world reliability, or safety in nearby worlds. "Guaranteed... whatever reality is like" is an infallibilist standard nobody here holds. **Fix:** show it bears on safety, or admit it does not engage externalism.

## 4. Does the formal import add anything?

**4a. SERIOUS. The results are largely analytic once imported.** Whole-record rivals are *defined* as experientially equivalent, so no experience separates them by definition (02:52-58). The second result is Putnam's 1963 diagonal argument and Gold/Kelly learning theory, none cited. **Fix:** cite them, and name one conclusion the formalism *yields* that the informal literature lacks.

**4b. SERIOUS. The Swinburne reply undercuts itself** (04:29-39, 05:79-82). The paper concedes that the invariance theorem *does* separate accounts whose lengths differ greatly. It never argues that theism and naturalism are near-tied. If Swinburne is right that theism is far shorter, or Dawkins is right that it is far longer, simplicity decides. "Reasonable change of language" (02:72) assumes what it needs. Language-relativity is an old objection (Goodman), which Swinburne answers in *Simplicity as Evidence of Truth* (1997). **Fix:** argue for the near-tie, and cite the invariance theorem and Swinburne 1997.

**4c. SERIOUS. The "uniform grid" is a reorganisation, and it is not uniform** (05:140-152). Each standard is "answered" by the result built to match it. Practical warrant and contextualism come out *compatible* (05:146-147), and reliability grants possible knowledge. That contradicts the abstract's "every route falls short under every standard" (abstract 31). The abstract says "nine routes" but §4 has eleven subsections, and the "route 2/route 3" numbering at 07:40 and 07:48 matches nothing. **Fix:** call the grid a heuristic and correct the abstract and numbering.

## 5. Tone

**MINOR to SERIOUS.** The scrutiny is uneven. Joseph Smith gets four accounts examined (04:126), Christianity one verse pair, and the "Satanic verses" get no reply from the tradition. Benson 2006 (03:81-84) is a null *behavioural* result, not measure-equivalence, and reads as a jab. For atheists, the evidential problem of evil, a clear case of the record "ruling some out", is absent. Naturalism is treated as a positive source-claim, with no engagement of methodological naturalism or Oppy's simpler-default view. **Fix:** even treatment, add evil, drop or recast STEP.

## 6. Other requirements

- **SERIOUS. Missing literature.** van Fraassen (constructive empiricism). Laudan & Leplin 1991 and Stanford 2006 (underdetermination). Chalmers 2005 and 2022 (on the simulation hypothesis as a *non-sceptical* metaphysical hypothesis, which bears directly on 1a). Wright 2004 (entitlement) and Pritchard (quasi-fideism), on which "imposed constraints" have positive status. Bergmann 2006. Kelly 1996. Zagzebski 2012. DeRose 2018 is misdescribed (07:69): his case is not confined to religious experience and is already symmetric between theist and atheist, which shrinks the novelty further.
- **MINOR. Clarity.** Three senses of "known", but "established" does the work (08:18-24). Pick one.
- **MINOR. Length.** About 11.3k words. Cut §2 (largely repeated at 03:51-74) and §5.4-5.6.

## Recommendation: **Major revision**, verging on reject

The three most important revisions:
1. **Fix the behaviour/source distinction (1a, 1c).** Defend source-level scepticism as general anti-realism (engaging van Fraassen and Chalmers), or show non-arbitrarily why the realist's table survives and theism does not. Recast "source" to respect primary and secondary causation.
2. **Restate the contribution honestly (3a, 4a-c).** Credit Alston, Putnam, Gold and Kelly. Drop the second-order and iʿjāz novelty claims. Argue the near-tie or drop the Swinburne reply. Correct "every standard" in the abstract.
3. **Engage the traditions on their strongest ground (2a-c).** Cover classical theism's necessity claims, the non-counterfeitable experiences (John of the Cross, Ignatius, Teresa, *ʿiṣma*), and the Catholic and Calvinist definitions of faith. Remove placeholders and correct quotations.
